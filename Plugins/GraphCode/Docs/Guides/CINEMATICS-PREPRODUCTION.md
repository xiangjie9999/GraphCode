# Plan and document the cinematic as a director

For AI cinematic work, prepare a saved director's packet before production authoring. Own the
audience experience, artistic decisions and relationships between departments. Explain what each
shot contributes, resolve consequential unknowns, and choose a coherent sequence before spending
effort on finished cameras, performance, assets or renders.

Use [presentation intent](CINEMATICS-INTENT.md), [artistic direction](CINEMATICS-DIRECTION.md),
[storyboards and view studies](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md), and
[audience attention](CINEMATICS-ATTENTION.md) for the craft decisions. This chapter defines their
records, order and readiness conditions. It specializes [RESEARCH-AND-PLAN.md](RESEARCH-AND-PLAN.md).

Professional practice informs this workflow: Disney's
[layout account](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/) connects edited boards to rough
3D staging and later camera refinement; Adobe's
[filmmaker interviews](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/shot-list.html) emphasize
usable coverage planning and adaptable shot records. The packet below is our recommended workflow,
not a claim that every studio uses the same documents.

## Direct the result, then organize its construction

Decide the sequence's purpose and the audience's knowledge before choosing a list of techniques.
Direct actors through playable intentions and relationships. Direct the camera through viewpoint,
attention and change. Direct editing and sound through progression, contrast and timing. Give
lighting, effects and production design a place in those decisions before they become finishing work.

A director's note states an observable problem and intended effect: "The tram departure is missed,
so the kindness appears costless; preserve the release gesture and departure in one readable beat."
It leaves room to compare suitable constructions. "Make it more epic" does not tell production
what must change or provide a useful review criterion.

Make choices within the authorized brief. Ask only when a missing user decision materially changes
the result and cannot be resolved from the project. A documented internal readiness gate is not an
extra permission checkpoint. When implementation is authorized and the packet is ready, proceed.

## Save the director's packet

Create a sequence-specific folder in the project's established planning area. Link its records
from a short index; keep stable sequence, beat, shot and revision IDs throughout. The names below
are illustrative. Combine short records when useful, but preserve every relevant decision and link.

| Record | Required content | What makes it usable |
|---|---|---|
| Brief and treatment | Audience, purpose, viewpoint, information progression, tone, art direction, scope and delivery | Specific audience changes with visible/audible evidence and success criteria. |
| Reference breakdown | Relevant passages/images/sounds, source, observed choice and intended adaptation | Explains what is learned and why it fits; distinguishes observation from a guessed setup. |
| Beat and attention sheet | Before/after knowledge, intention, primary/secondary/withheld information, action and sound | Each beat has a meaningful change; each transition has a reason. |
| Staging and continuity plan | Overhead geography, performer/prop paths, eyelines, axes, scale, entrances and exits | The action can occur in a consistent space and the important relationships can be photographed. |
| Visual boards and studies | Readable panels, shot IDs, key changes, camera/action notation, candidate captures and selection notes | Actual images communicate the planned views; studies answer named questions. |
| Animatic and sound plan | Continuous timed edit, frame ledger, dialogue/essential cues, pauses, sound perspective, transitions and tails | Timing has been watched and heard; frame rate, master/source mapping, temporary elements and limitations are identified. |
| Shot and transition records | Purpose, entry/turn/exit, framing, move/focus cues, selected technique/alternative/reason, cut logic, ranges/handles and continuity | A production author can explain the choice, build each shot and join it to its neighbors. |
| Production dependency plan | Assets, performances, contacts, lights/FX/audio, GraphCode owners, native handoffs, delivery and runtime needs | Each necessary mechanism has a verified route and an inspection method. |
| Decisions and review log | Alternatives, selected approach, reasons, evidence, unresolved items and changed dependencies | Review can trace a choice to the brief and distinguish planned, observed and finished work. |

Scale the packet to the task: a short product reveal may use a concise brief, a few panels, a
short timing study and a compact dependency table. A character sequence needs more performance
and continuity detail. Mark an inapplicable item with its reason; do not silently omit it.
More pages are not proof of better direction.

Save image, audio and video evidence with stable links and revision labels. A document saying
"storyboard done" without viewable panels does not supply a storyboard. An unplayed shot table
does not supply timing evidence. If a necessary tool or asset is unavailable, record the limit
and the next distinguishing check rather than claim the stage passed.

## Work through the decisions in order

### 1. Establish context and purpose

Read the request, art direction, adjacent scenes and available assets. Inspect the relevant space
and delivery constraints. Write the [brief](CINEMATICS-INTENT.md#write-a-brief-another-author-can-use)
and treatment, including what the sequence inherits and leaves behind. Separate facts, creative
choices and assumptions. Keep a consequential question visible until evidence resolves it.

### 2. Research what determines the result

Study complete relevant passages, including their sound, rather than collect impressive camera
stills. Explain how information, action, performance and cuts create the effect. Use current
technical help/source for the intended GraphCode route. Research only the uncertainties that
affect this sequence; the generic guide does not establish that a particular setup works here.

### 3. Design beats, staging and attention

Write the opening, meaningful changes and payoff. Map geography and action paths; decide who
knows what. Name each beat's primary attention and necessary evidence. Develop thumbnail alternatives
for consequential choices such as viewpoint, reveal order or whether an action remains in one shot.
Use [shot design](CINEMATICS-SHOTS.md) and [attention planning](CINEMATICS-ATTENTION.md).

Apply [expressive technique selection](CINEMATICS-TECHNIQUE-SELECTION.md) explicitly. Connect
intent to evidence, staging/viewpoint, camera, edit and sound; compare meaningful alternatives
instead of assuming the AI will infer the right combination from individual technique definitions.

### 4. Resolve visual uncertainty with inexpensive studies

Before a study, save its question, intent, candidates, constants, comparison criteria and stopping
condition. Rough stills or short camera passes are valid ways to discover the space before final
boards and shot selection. Use simple assets sufficient for the question; preserve relevant scale,
silhouette, action and light relationships. Follow the
[view-study procedure](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md#run-simple-view-studies-before-selecting-final-shots).

Document the selected approach and why the others were rejected. If none works, name the cause
and revise the staging or hypothesis before the next test. Do not polish a candidate merely
because it was the first one implemented.

### 5. Board and time the whole passage

Turn the choices into [visual panels and a timed animatic](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md). Include
performance changes, camera starts/landings, audience focus, cut logic and important sound. Review
the continuous sequence without explaining it aloud. Record what was inspected and what remains
an assumption; if a fresh viewer is unavailable, label the self-review accordingly.

First [review key frames and record their timing](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md#review-key-frames-before-final-recording),
then test the animatic and rough continuous layout. Keep frame rate, master/source ranges, cue
frames and separate picture/sound boundaries explicit. Update them after a consequential retime.

Revise redundant views, unclear causes, weak decisions and rushed information before detailed
production. A pacing change can require a new performance or camera path; carry that change into
the shot and dependency records.

### 6. Resolve the production route

Map each planned view and cue to its assets and owner using
[CINEMATICS-GRAPHCODE.md](CINEMATICS-GRAPHCODE.md). Identify required actors/components, sequence
bindings, performance/root/contact ownership, sound placement, simulation history, native features
and output/runtime verification. Use current help and canonical reads; a feature's name is not proof
that all its controls are exposed.

Test a risky binding, performance interaction or transition with the smallest representative
setup before scheduling finished work that depends on it. Inspect actual playback when timing or
runtime behavior is the question. A source audit, still or successful apply has a narrower scope.
Record an explicit native handoff or supported alternative for each uncarried feature.

### 7. Freeze a production baseline and build from it

Resolve the [readiness conditions](#check-readiness-before-production), save the packet revision
and order construction by dependencies. Finish a representative difficult passage before scaling
its approach across the sequence. Track changes against the baseline and review the entire edit
as individual shots mature. A baseline organizes work; evidence can reopen a decision.

## Keep decisions and tests concrete

Use a short record for each consequential choice:

```text
Decision ID / related beat, shot and packet revision:
Intended audience effect and necessary evidence:
Known facts / assumptions / unanswered question:
Alternatives and the meaningful difference between them:
Smallest study or research that distinguishes them:
Constants / comparison criteria / stopping condition:
Result: what was actually inspected, evidence link and limits.
Selection and reason; rejected alternatives and cause.
Affected boards, timing, assets and technical dependencies:
Next action; condition that would reopen the decision:
```

Original courier example: compare a shared view of help/departure with a closer hesitation view
and a connected departure cut. Keep the action and sound cue comparable. Evaluate whether the
decision reads, the cost is perceived, and the chosen treatment stays restrained. If the shared
view hides the hand, change staging or select the connected coverage for that reason. Do not call
either alternative superior until it has been inspected in context.

Record technical unknowns similarly: question, prerequisite, distinguishing test, acceptance,
owner and evidence. Distinguish a decision intentionally postponed until final performance from
a missing mechanism on which production already depends. Resolve a blocking unknown before the
dependent production step; document nonblocking uncertainty and when it must be settled.

## Check readiness before production

The AI performs and records this review. It should not silently switch from exploration into
finished production because a single candidate looks promising.

- The brief and treatment define audience, purpose, viewpoint, art direction, delivery and scope.
- Beats have perceptible changes and a coherent progression; primary attention is planned.
- Staging and continuity are mapped; exploratory views have recorded results and selections.
- Actual boards show the necessary changes; the timed passage and essential sound have been reviewed.
- Each shot and transition has a purpose, selected technique/alternative/reason, cue, entry/exit and relationship to its neighbors.
- Required assets, performance, contact, simulation, sound and lighting dependencies have owners.
- Consequential GraphCode/native mechanisms have a checked route; blocking unknowns are resolved.
- Construction order, representative quality test, review criteria and delivery/runtime verification plan are saved.
- The packet's baseline, evidence limits and remaining scheduled decisions are explicit.

If a condition fails, repair the corresponding plan, board, study or mechanism before spending
production effort on that dependency. Continue independent authorized work. Do not replace the
missing decision with repeated parameter changes or finish more shots around an unresolved core.

## Give production useful notes and revise deliberately

Review the audience effect first, then its cause, then a proposed correction. For example:
"The gaze cue arrives after the cut, so the reveal feels unrelated; compare a later cut with an
earlier gaze cue, keeping the reveal framing constant." Change one meaningful cause when possible.

Classify the note: intent, staging/performance, attention, camera, edit/sound, finish or integration.
Fix it where it originates. An unclear character decision may need different behavior rather
than extra depth of field; weak geography may need staging rather than another dissolve.

When evidence changes the plan, record the reason and revise dependent boards, timing and asset
records together. Describe the current decision confidently while keeping the evidence honest.
The director remains responsible for the complete sequence through
[final review and delivery](CINEMATICS.md#review-gates-before-calling-it-finished).
