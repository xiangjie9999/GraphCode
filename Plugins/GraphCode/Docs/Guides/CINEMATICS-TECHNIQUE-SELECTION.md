# Choose techniques for the intended presentation

Choose a technique because it makes the intended action, information or relationship perceptible
in the project's artistic language. Write that reason before production. Knowing how to author
a pan, close-up or dissolve does not establish when it belongs in this sequence.

Use [intent](CINEMATICS-INTENT.md) to define the experience and
[direction](CINEMATICS-DIRECTION.md) to develop the treatment. This chapter connects those choices
to [shots](CINEMATICS-SHOTS.md), [movement](CINEMATICS-CAMERA-MOVEMENT.md),
[editing/transitions](CINEMATICS-EDITING-AND-TRANSITIONS.md) and
[attention](CINEMATICS-ATTENTION.md). Their chapters own the detailed execution procedures.

## Connect the director's plan to boards and tests

The [director's packet](CINEMATICS-PREPRODUCTION.md) records purpose, decisions and production
dependencies. [Boards and previs](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md) test those decisions through images,
sound and time. A board can expose a better choice; record the reason and revise the plan. A written
intention does not prove its effect, and an attractive capture does not explain why it belongs.

Professional accounts demonstrate this relationship. ASC's
[movement discussion](https://theasc.com/article/shot-craft-camera-movement/) begins with the script
and director's intent. Pixar's
[Incredibles 2 account](https://renderman.pixar.com/stories/incredible-cinematography) describes
a shared visual language, contrasting camera treatments and early contextual collaboration.
The comparisons below are original recommendations informed by those practices, not universal
studio formulas or claims about a completed audience test.

## Make the decision in order

1. **Name the audience change.** What should become understood, anticipated, felt or newly important?
   Describe the before/after state and whose experience guides it.
2. **Name the evidence and information order.** What behavior, object, relationship or sound makes
   that change possible? Who knows first? What must remain available or withheld?
3. **Stage it in a coherent space.** Place the performers, props, paths and gaze targets. Decide
   whether the audience needs a shared relationship, an isolated response or a new spatial view.
4. **Choose framing and camera behavior.** Compare a hold, motivated move and cut where relevant.
   Choose distance/lens/focus around the necessary relationship and the established treatment.
5. **Choose the edit and sound relationship.** Decide entry, reading time, reaction, exit, temporal
   relation and independent sound cues. A sound bridge can supply continuity without a visual blend.
6. **Specify the test and record the selection.** Use key frames for composition, a timed animatic
   for progression, and rough continuous layout for motion/space. Review the affected neighbors.
   Record why the selected construction fits, what was observed and what remains unproven.

If step 2 has no evidence, more elaborate camera work will not supply it automatically. If step 3
cannot show the necessary relationship, restage or change coverage before polishing the move.
Carry the selected rationale into the shot record and [GraphCode asset plan](CINEMATICS-GRAPHCODE.md).

## Start from the expressive presentation

Treat these as candidate constructions to evaluate, not presets attached to emotion words:

| Presentation intent | Necessary relationship | Candidate language | What would undermine it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intimacy, listening or a private decision | Small behavior, time to think, meaningful distance between people | Shared held frame, restrained proximity change, selective listener coverage, breathing/silence in the mix | A move or cut that hides the thought; isolating every speaker so the relationship disappears. |
| Suspense and anticipation | Clear difference between audience and character knowledge, with a readable possible consequence | Hold on unresolved space, off-screen cue, controlled reveal, reaction or cross-cut when the strands warrant it | Revealing the answer early; withholding basic geography so the audience cannot anticipate anything. |
| Shock, rupture or discovery | An established expectation and a distinct new fact | Sudden reveal/cut, changed sound space, brief energetic reframing followed by a readable arrival | Simultaneous blur, noise and cuts that prevent recognition of what changed. |
| Calm observation or contemplation | Duration, relationships and sensory detail worth attending to | Stable or gently developing composition, motivated background life, sustained ambience and selective cuts | Interchangeable beauty views with no progression; constant movement preventing observation. |
| Comic setup and reversal | An understandable pattern, failure of expectation and time for response | Planted geography, a held action/reaction, a purposeful delayed or abrupt cut, selective sonic punctuation | Unclear setup, camera announcing the gag too early, or cutting before its consequence registers. |
| Grandeur, scale or purposeful exploration | Familiar scale anchors and changing spatial relationships | Wide relationships, reveal through depth, motivated crane/tracking, contrasts between detail and extent | An unanchored aerial view, repetitive large reveals, or travel that loses the destination. |
| Product identity and desirability | Characteristic form, material, silhouette and coherent presentation | Controlled light/reflection, purposeful orbit or translation, clear hero composition and restrained detail views | A move that hides identifying features, unstable reflections or an unrelated visual flourish. |
| Product function or mechanism clarity | Cause, contact, movement and result in a readable relationship | Stable explanatory framing, deeper readable space, action/detail coverage and synchronized functional sound | Shallow focus or a beauty orbit hiding the operation; an edit that omits decisive contact. |
| Trailer escalation or thematic montage | A promise developed through meaningful contrast and selected information | Varied energy, scale and rhythm; purposeful associations and sound progression | Starting at maximum intensity and staying there; withholding the premise along with every answer. |
| Passage of time, memory or association | A legible temporal or conceptual relationship | Direct ellipsis, graphic/sound connection, dissolve or fade when their overlap/punctuation serves it | A default dissolve that confuses time/space, or a clever match with no useful meaning. |

Light, palette, texture and performance must support the selected language. Preserve the project's
style rather than borrow an isolated genre cue. A graphic, brightly staged suspense scene may use
different contrast and camera behavior from a dim naturalistic one while preserving the same
audience knowledge relationship.

## Compare neighboring techniques explicitly

| Choice | Choose it when this relationship matters | Choose another construction when… | Decisive review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold vs push vs closer cut | Hold lets action/thought develop in the existing relationship; push changes proximity continuously; cut selects a new emphasis immediately | The gesture is unreadable from the current view, continuous approach adds empty time, or the cut interrupts a necessary thought | Does the change in proximity/emphasis arrive with the beat and preserve its evidence? |
| Pan vs cut to the destination | Pan can make looking and spatial connection part of the discovery; cut can prioritize the new fact or response immediately | The passage has no useful information, an obstacle blocks the pivot, or a cut would destroy an important shared-space relation | Can the audience connect cue and target, and does revelation occur at the intended time? |
| Tilt vs vertical travel | Tilt redirects a stationary view vertically; travel changes height and depth relationships | Rotation cannot reveal the surface or vantage needed, or translation adds irrelevant parallax | Inspect the intermediate relationship and the meaningful arrival, not only the end frame. |
| Tracking vs a planted frame | Tracking accompanies a person/action and controls changing background relations; a planted view lets travel alter the composition | Following hides the destination/consequence, or the static view loses an essential gesture | Do route, relative movement and the intended audience alignment remain legible? |
| Orbit vs selected angles | Orbit reveals changing sides and relationships continuously; selected angles can prioritize particular evidence | The orbit spends time on an uninformative side, reverses screen direction confusingly, or hides a product interface | Does every useful passage add information, and can the key interaction still be read? |
| Crane/reveal vs grounded wide | Changing height/depth can discover scope; a grounded wide can preserve human scale and immediacy | The large move disconnects the audience from the subject or shows the payoff before it is earned | Is scale established through recognizable relationships, and does the reveal develop the intended idea? |
| Push vs zoom | Translation changes perspective/parallax; zoom changes field of view from the same position | The choice depends on a spatial change the other cannot supply, or its visible operation conflicts with the treatment | Compare the subject/background relationship through the whole move. |
| Rack focus vs readable shared depth | Rack focus transfers sharpness between available planes; greater depth preserves simultaneous relationships | A critical action must be read in both planes, a target is too small, or the transfer arrives before its cue | Can the audience perceive the intended relation without losing necessary evidence? |
| Handheld/operator response vs controlled stability | Designed operator response can share urgency, pursuit or observation; stability can let uncertainty unfold inside the frame | Motion destroys geography or small performance, or its intensity has no relationship to the scene's established language | Watch for meaning and readability at delivery speed; generic noise is not an operator's intention. |
| Straight cut vs dissolve vs fade | Cut replaces the view; dissolve creates image overlap; fade supplies a passage through a solid field | Overlap creates competing subjects, punctuation implies a break the story does not need, or the cut loses an intended association | Does the boundary communicate the intended time/idea relationship with readable images and sound? |
| Continuity/action cut vs conceptual match | Continuity preserves an event; a match can connect separate moments through image, movement or sound | The resemblance makes a new time/place look like the same physical action, or matching form conceals a needed causal beat | What will the audience infer about time, place and causality? |
| J/L sound bridge vs simultaneous picture/sound change | Incoming sound can prepare a view; outgoing sound can keep a response connected; simultaneous change can punctuate a break | The bridge spoils withheld information, suggests a false sound source, masks a response or breaks visible speech sync | Listen and watch across the whole overlap and tail. |
| Cross-cutting vs staying with one strand | Interleaving can build a meaningful relationship or anticipation between established actions | It invents unsupported simultaneity/deadlines or interrupts the stronger developing performance | Does returning to each strand change expectation while preserving the actual event relationships? |
| Hidden/whip seam vs a visible cut | Concealed continuity or a conspicuous attention snap serves the presentation | Concealment adds difficulty without meaning, breaks motion/lighting, or sacrifices a readable arrival | Test the actual moving seam and its landing; endpoints cannot establish continuity. |

Use the detailed [move procedures](CINEMATICS-CAMERA-MOVEMENT.md) and
[transition procedures](CINEMATICS-EDITING-AND-TRANSITIONS.md) after making the choice. A native
camera blend interpolates a viewpoint; it is not the image overlap of a dissolve. Check
[GraphCode/native coverage](CINEMATICS-GRAPHCODE.md#author-camera-moves-and-editorial-transitions)
before choosing a construction whose controls are not carried by the installed authoring route.

## Coordinate the techniques across the sequence

Establish a pattern in distance, stability, duration, composition and sound perspective. Decide
where a departure matters: a first close-up, the camera leaving a person, a sudden stillness or an
ambience falling away can gain meaning through what precedes it. Constant intensity gives later
beats less room to change. Do not make every shot an independent climax.

Let techniques share a cause. A look may redirect attention, a pan may discover its destination,
then a held landing may let the discovery register. A reaction cut may instead carry the same
knowledge change more effectively. Sound can lead or delay either construction. Do not stack a
push, rack focus, lighting change, musical hit and cut merely because they are available; decide
which cue leads and which supports it.

An intentional change in style needs a relationship to the presentation and adjacent scenes.
Record whether the audience should feel continuity, escalation, release or rupture. A low angle,
warm palette, slow move or shallow plane has no fixed meaning independent of subject, behavior,
context and the established visual language.

## Worked decision: hold, push or cut on the courier's choice

Use [the courier dilemma](CINEMATICS.md#worked-scene-a-courier-chooses-to-help). The intended
evidence is voluntary redirection toward the fallen parcel while the departing tram gives that
choice a cost. Keep that action and sound relationship comparable in the following original study.

**Held shared view:** place the route, courier and person so the hesitation and redirected weight
read together. This candidate preserves the choice as a spatial relationship. It needs a gesture
large/clear enough for the chosen distance and delivery size. If the handoff is hidden, repair
staging or coverage; simply holding longer will not expose it.

**Restrained push:** begin from the established relationship and develop proximity as the courier
commits. This candidate can emphasize a developing thought if the performance supports it. Keep
the decision readable before the framing removes surrounding evidence; preserve the cost through
established geography, sound or connected coverage. Compare whether approach adds meaning or merely
delays the action.

**Cut to a closer response:** use the established gaze/action to motivate an immediate new emphasis,
then connect the redirection and consequence. This candidate can expose behavior too small for the
shared view. It needs coherent eyelines, action phase and time; if the cut makes the stop seem
involuntary or separates help from departure, revise the coverage or edit.

Board the decisive moments of each viable candidate. Compare a timed excerpt with sound, followed
by rough layout where movement/occlusion matters. Select against agency, perceived cost, treatment
and continuity. Document the observation that determined the choice rather than declare one camera
technique inherently more emotional.

## Worked decision: beauty presentation and functional explanation

For an original one-hand latch demonstration, separate identity from operation. A controlled orbit
may establish the object's form and material. The demonstration needs the occupied hand, free hand,
contact, latch travel and successful opening to remain understandable. A held oblique or profile
view may serve that action better; an insert is useful only if a small interface otherwise cannot read.

Plan a deliberate connection between hero view and explanatory view. Preserve the object's identity,
orientation and recognizable parts. Time the cut before the decisive contact or match the continuous
action with usable coverage. Place functional sound at its actual cause and allow the result to read.
Return to a hero composition only if it supplies the intended payoff.

Judge a reflective/material study through the intended camera; judge operation in continuous motion.
An attractive orbit and a successful isolated mechanism test do not prove their combined presentation.

## Worked decision: reveal scale through a pan, travel or cut

For an original observatory reveal, establish a person, entrance or path as a scale anchor. Decide
whether the audience should discover the building with the person or anticipate it before their arrival.

A pan from that anchor can preserve a pivot's spatial connection if the destination is visible from
there. Travel through foreground depth can progressively expose the structure if its occlusion and
parallax are useful. A cut to a wider relationship can prioritize the new scale immediately. A rising
camera needs a meaningful change in vantage rather than height for its own sake.

Compare start, partial discovery and arrival frames, then a timed passage. Inspect whether the anchor
remains useful, the destination arrives at the intended moment, and the move has an informative middle.
Use the soundtrack to support the revelation without masking orientation cues. Choose the construction
that serves the treatment and actual space; a bigger move is not a default improvement.

## Record the reason before production

In each consequential shot/transition record, write: **intended change; necessary evidence; selected
construction; meaningful alternative; why selected; study/review evidence and limits.** Link to the
[decision log](CINEMATICS-PREPRODUCTION.md#keep-decisions-and-tests-concrete) for unresolved questions.

Then use [frame-based board review](CINEMATICS-STORYBOARDS.md#review-key-frames-before-final-recording)
and a timed test to challenge the choice. Review the sequence without author narration. If the
intended effect is absent, identify whether evidence, staging, attention, performance, camera,
edit or sound caused the problem. Revise that cause and the linked records before final recording.
