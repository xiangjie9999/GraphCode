# Human movement: doors and devices

**What a correct-looking result is.** A person can reach the door hardware without the hand missing it. The hand
stays on the hardware as the door moves, and the body has room to pass. At a desk, the arms hang naturally, the
forearms and wrists stay aligned, the feet have support, and the screen fits a comfortable line of sight.
For a phone, distinguish a believable habitual downward look from a posture intended to keep neck effort low.

This guide gives the retained door design limits, observed phone postures and workstation recommendations.
The door dimensions do not establish a measured opening technique. The phone observations do not establish a
recommended neck pose. The workstation rows describe a good setup rather than measured typing movement.
([US Access Board, undated in the retained record, ADA standards][ADA];
[Lee, Kang & Shin, 2015, smartphone head flexion][Lee15];
[Namwongsa et al., 2019, smartphone neck posture][Namwongsa19];
[OSHA, undated in the retained record, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions].)

**Read the numbers correctly.** `C` means full text read in the retained research; `Ca`, abstract only.
`D` marks an inference or calculation from cited rows. The values and their source-specific units are preserved.
Angles are anatomical unless a row says otherwise. A neck angle whose definition was not retained cannot be
treated as head flexion from vertical, a joint limit, or a rig rotation. These sources were recorded on
2026-10-07; they have not been fetched again.

## The movement, in order

The following is an authoring checklist for the visible task, not a measured phase model. The retained door and
device sources supply no phase durations. **Unknown** means no usable timing, joint angle or distance was found;
it does not mean zero. Choose scene-specific performance timing and label it as authored.

### Open and pass through a door

| Phase to show | Contact or body action to inspect | Timing and measured posture |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Bring the body to a place from which the actual hardware is reachable; preserve the available clearance | Approach duration, step placement and trunk posture unknown |
| Reach and take the hardware | Hand arrives on the measured handle or push surface | Hand-to-handle timing, shoulder/elbow/wrist angles and reach distance unknown |
| Operate and move the door | Coordinate hand contact, door movement and the body | Push/pull trunk lean, foot placement, arm posture and opening duration unknown |
| Pass through | Clear the moving door with the body and any held object | Passing steps, distances and timing unknown |
| Let go and continue | End the hand contact at the visible release event | Release timing and recovery posture unknown |

This sequence does not claim that a person uses one fixed stance or keeps the same grip for every door. A push
plate, lever and pull handle need different contact targets. No measured push/pull technique is supplied here.
The design dimensions below come from [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA] (`C`).

### Use a phone

| Phase to show | What to author or inspect | Timing and measured posture |
|---|---|---|
| Bring the phone into use | Establish its hand attachment, grip and readable orientation | Acquisition timing, wrist angle, viewing distance and thumb posture unknown |
| Look at the display | Coordinate the head, eyes and phone height | Head/neck observations below are task postures, not a measured transition curve |
| Text, browse or watch | Retain the grip while the working digits and attention change | Tap cadence, thumb trajectories, per-action duration and movement phases unknown |
| Finish or put it away | End the task and transition the grip and attention deliberately | Release, lowering and recovery timings unknown |

Lee compares texting, browsing and video. Namwongsa measures muscle activity during texting. Neither gives a
complete acquisition-to-stow timing model. The **1.5 min** in Namwongsa is the observation duration, not a phone-use
animation target. ([Lee, Kang & Shin, 2015, smartphone head flexion][Lee15], `Ca`;
[Namwongsa et al., 2019, smartphone neck posture][Namwongsa19], `Ca`.)

For general look-before-hand coordination, use [HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md](HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md). Its other tasks'
timings do not fill the missing phone-specific timings.

### Work at a keyboard

| Phase to show | What to author or inspect | Timing and measured posture |
|---|---|---|
| Settle at the desk | Place chair, feet, keyboard and screen so the body can take the recommended setup | Setup duration and transition angles unknown |
| Bring hands to the keys | Let upper arms hang naturally; align forearms and wrists | Reach duration and measured approach trajectory unknown |
| Type and view the screen | Keep the setup below; author the actual finger actions and attention | Measured keypress phases, typing cadence, wrist extension, shoulder abduction and neck flexion unknown |
| Pause or leave | Change support and attention with the task | Pause spacing and exit timing unknown |

These are setup checks based on [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] and
[OSHA, workstation monitors][OSHAmonitors] (`C`). OSHA's guidance does not supply a measured typing cycle.
For sitting and getting up, use [HUMAN-SIT-STAND-FLOOR.md](HUMAN-SIT-STAND-FLOOR.md).

## Angles and distances

### Door geometry and force

| Where it matters | Value exactly as retained | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Reaching the hardware | Hardware height between **34 in (865 mm)** and **48 in (1220 mm)** above the floor | [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA] (`C`); section number not retained |
| Moving an interior door | Opening force **not more than 5 lb (22.2 N)** | [US Access Board, ADA standards, Table 404.2.9 as recorded][ADA] (`C`). A design limit, not a measured person's push force |
| Pull-side space | Clearance **at least 18 in (455 mm)** | [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA] (`C`); approach conditions and section number not retained. This single dimension is not a complete clearance layout |
| Forward reach envelope | High reach **48 in (1220 mm) at most**; low reach **15 in (380 mm) at least** | [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA] (`C`); section number and detailed conditions not retained. Not a hand path or a character-specific reach distance |

The imperial and metric figures are the source's paired values. Do not recalculate one to replace the other.
These rows are the limits recorded in the research, not a complete door standard or a universal biomechanical
stance. They supply no hand force curve, trunk lean, foot spacing or joint-angle sequence.

### Phone observations and the low-load comparison

| Task or condition | Value exactly as retained | Evidence and angle convention |
|---|---|---|
| Texting, browsing and video | Head flexion from vertical **33–45° (50th percentile)**; largest for texting, larger sitting than standing | **18 people**. [Lee, Kang & Shin, 2015, smartphone head flexion][Lee15] (`Ca`). Head from vertical is explicitly defined |
| Standing | Neck flexion, women **69.0°**, men **76.1°** | **32 adults, 16 M / 16 F**. [Chen et al., 2024, standing and sitting smartphone posture][PhoneListing] (`Ca`, *Scientific Reports*). The neck-flexion definition was not retained |
| Unsupported sitting | Neck flexion, women **71.8°**, men **76.1°** | Same study and undefined convention. [Chen et al., 2024, standing and sitting smartphone posture][PhoneListing] (`Ca`) |
| Standing and walking, with and without backpack loads | Head angle **19.3° (men)** against **14.6° (women)**; upper trunk **28.8°** against **26.4°** | **30 students**, motion capture. [Chen, Tang & Guan, 2025, phone posture and backpack loads][PhoneListing] (`Ca`). Definitions not retained |
| Virtual classroom, by device | Neck angle: smartphone **32.48 ± 11.81°**, tablet **36.93 ± 7.97°**, notebook **39.30 ± 7.82°** | **24 women aged 18–23**. [Intaruk & Sengsoon, 2025, device posture in a virtual classroom][PhoneListing] (`Ca`). Angle definition not retained |
| Phone use while walking | Most users have gaze angle **above 60°**; men have larger neck flexion, gaze angle and viewing distance | **120 users, 60 M / 60 F**. [Chen, Wang, Chang & Nguyen, 2024, walking smartphone posture][PhoneListing] (`Ca`). Definitions and viewing-distance values not retained |
| Lower neck-muscle load while texting | Muscle activity described as acceptably low at neck flexion **0–15°** | **44 people**, texting for **1.5 min**. [Namwongsa et al., 2019, smartphone neck posture][Namwongsa19] (`Ca`). An activity result, not a prescribed wrist/thumb grip or whole-body posture |

**Keep the angle definitions separate.** The **33–45°** head-from-vertical measurement cannot be averaged with
**69.0° / 76.1°** neck flexion, the **19.3° / 14.6°** head angles, or the device neck-angle figures. The latter
definitions were not retained. Do not infer a conversion from how similar or different the values look.
Sources and tasks are in the table.

For an ergonomic setup, use the low-load neck finding as a comparison and bring the screen to a usable view without
inventing a phone height or wrist angle. For an observed habitual performance, keep the chosen study's convention
and task. Do not describe its large habitual flexion as the recommended pose.
([Namwongsa et al., 2019, smartphone neck posture][Namwongsa19];
[Lee, Kang & Shin, 2015, smartphone head flexion][Lee15].)

Phone use on stairs reduces cadence and speed in the retained study. Use
[HUMAN-STAIRS-AND-LADDERS.md](HUMAN-STAIRS-AND-LADDERS.md) for those measurements; this page adds no slowdown
factor. ([Di Giulio et al., 2020, phone use on stairs][DiGiulio20], `Ca`.)

### Keyboard and screen setup

| Body part or relationship | Recommended setup exactly as retained | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows | Bent between **90 and 120°** | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`) |
| Upper arms | Hang normally at the side of the body | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`) |
| Wrists and forearms | Straight, in line, roughly parallel to the floor | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`); no measured wrist-extension angle supplied |
| Head and neck | Level, forward facing, balanced, generally in line with the torso | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`); no measured typing neck-flexion angle supplied |
| Thighs and hips | Supported, generally parallel to the floor | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`) |
| Knees and feet | Knees about as high as hips, feet slightly forward; feet fully supported by floor or footrest | [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions] (`C`) |
| Eye to screen | Between **20 and 40 in (50–100 cm)** | [OSHA, workstation monitors][OSHAmonitors] (`C`) |
| Screen height | Top at or slightly below eye level; centre normally **15 to 20° below horizontal eye level** | [OSHA, workstation monitors][OSHAmonitors] (`C`) |
| Downward viewing angle | **Never greater than 60°** in any of the **four reference postures** | [OSHA, workstation monitors][OSHAmonitors] (`C`). A viewing-angle limit, not a recommended head tilt |

The elbow range is a setup recommendation. It is not a joint's full anatomical range or a requirement that the elbow
move from one endpoint to the other during each keypress. Screen-centre viewing angle is not neck flexion.

## The most common mistakes

These are checks inferred from the cited setup and observation rows, not separately measured error frequencies.

1. **Hardware outside the recorded accessible height band.** Check the door geometry table before compensating with
   an extreme arm pose. [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA].
2. **A guessed door force becomes a body-lean recipe.** The opening-force limit supplies neither a person's actual
   force nor their posture. Preserve the push/pull gap. [US Access Board, ADA standards][ADA].
3. **Large phone flexion is called the comfortable target.** The habitual head-angle study and the lower-load
   muscle-activity result answer different questions. [Lee, Kang & Shin, 2015, smartphone head flexion][Lee15];
   [Namwongsa et al., 2019, smartphone neck posture][Namwongsa19].
4. **Elbows fall outside the recommended desk range, or wrists bend away from the forearms.** Check the whole arm
   and keyboard placement together. [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions].
5. **The screen demands an exaggerated downward look or the feet hang without support.** Check monitor distance and
   height, then chair and foot support. [OSHA, workstation monitors][OSHAmonitors];
   [OSHA, computer workstation positions][OSHApositions].

### A door the hand opens (built 2026-10-24)

The door moves along its OWN clip and the hand moves it there. Write two key poses -- the hand on the handle of the
shut door, and the same hand on the handle of the door as far open as it should go -- and travel between them: both
keys hold the handle, so the arm's way between them runs along the handle's arc and the door turns to the second key
with nothing tuned. Fixture: `Content/GraphCodeExamples/HouseDoor_20261024/` -- `HouseDoorSwing.gcmove` (the door's
travel), `HouseDoorKeys.gcmove` and `HouseDoorKeysOpen.gcmove` (the keys), `HouseDoorOpen.gcmove` (the person) and
`BP_HouseDoorPlayback` (plays both). Record: `Docs/GC-MOVE-MOVING-PARTS-DONE.md`, "D6: a person opens a house door".

1. **The door's file** is a reference: `character:` + `part:<the door's mesh component>`, an empty `build { }`, and one
   clip whose first and last frames are its stops: `Swing = clip(frames=60) { swing(Door, axis=Z, by=$Opening) }`.
   Build the door's mesh on its hinge (`.gcshape` `[pivot=Hinge]`) so its own Z is the hinge line.
2. **What the hand touches is the door's collision** (`surface(..., solid=collision)`), so give the leaf and its handle
   convex solids that do not touch (`.gcshape` `collision=($Slab, $Rose, $Ball, ...)`; a round knob's ball is a convex
   profile of its own). Measured on the fixture: the drawn mesh as the hand's solid took 7-236 s a sample, and a
   whole-body `collision($Door)` against a door one sample past four minutes. The grab is the contact a key needs.
3. **Key one, the shut door:** `Door = surface(part=Door, solid=collision, along=/Game/.../HouseDoorSwing.Swing)`;
   `reach(limb_l, from=$PalmL, to=$Grasp, weight=$Held)` with `PalmL = frame(socket=palm_grip_l, toward=X, up=Z)` and
   a palm frame on the handle's socket, and `grab(fingers_l, on=$Door, contacts=auto, weight=$Closes)`, in a short clip
   (10 frames) so the fingers close sample by sample. **Stand him with the knob in front of the hand's own side**, just
   outside the shoulder line and about 35 cm out (on the fixture 29 cm left of his centre, 34 out), and far enough to the
   latch side that the leaf's free edge, swinging toward him, passes outside the knee nearest it once that knee bends over
   the toes (at 24 cm left the edge went 0.4 cm into his left knee mid-pull; the build's final check carries the door along
   its clip and refused it, 2026-10-25; 5 cm further over, he stands 2 cm nearer so the arm still reaches). Beside the hip the
   forearm hangs straight down onto it and the wrist sits at its stop -- the user, on that video: *"do you think the
   hand is even natural?"*; in front of the belly a forearm coming along the knob runs through it. **Take a round knob
   FROM ABOVE, half-turned:** palm over the top of the ball, turned about 30 degrees off palm-down toward the thumb
   (palm-down puts the forearm's twist at its stop), fingers pointing on toward the door and turned 15 degrees in the
   palm's plane (straight, the wrist's side bend sat at its stop), the ball under the BASE of the fingers (the palm's
   centre 2 cm out from the ball's widest ring, 0.6-0.9 off its top): the forearm then slopes down along the knob with
   the wrist a little extended, which is how a knob is turned (Timm et al. 1993: forearm rotation, wrist extended). On
   the fixture's Knob socket (+X up the door, +Z out of it): `at=(3.14, -2.16, 2.4), toward=(-0.866, 0.5, 0),
   up=(-0.129, -0.224, -0.966)`. **Close the thumb WITH the fingers:** a grip of the four finger chains and the thumb
   (`fist_l = grip(index_l, middle_l, ring_l, pinky_l, thumb_l)`), the thumb started spread (thumb_01 fold +50), the
   grab's weight about 0.7 (closed fully, this body's pinky stops in its ring finger: its finger hulls overlap at rest).
   A thumb outside the grip rests on the ball and nothing can fit it (refused at its start, or carried into the ball as
   the hand settles). **`around=` cannot free a knob's roll:** on a socket of a part it turns round the PART's own axis
   through its origin (the cup's rule) -- on a door leaf, the hinge; the build's own "grip offset" note shows where the
   hand really went. (Superseded 2026-10-25: palm against the ball's end, fingers hanging down, `at=(0, 0, 2.2),
   toward=-Z, up=-X`, which only suits a forearm hanging straight down beside the knob.)
4. **Key two, the door part-open, with the body in the pull:** the same surface line with `at=` -- a time of the door's
   own clip (`at=0.45`, `frame(27)`, `0.9s`) -- stands the door there for everything in that file, its sockets and its
   collision. Start from key one, `pose(source=<key one>, from=reference) { }`, then write the BODY opening the door,
   ground up: the hips back and down over the feet (`shift(root, toward=back, by=12)`, `down` 12) -- NOT sideways: with
   each foot held where it stood, a hip moved sideways can only slant the shins over flat feet, and the ankles roll (the
   user, 2026-10-25: *"why the ankle bent down?"*) -- the knees said forward over the toes (`aim(thigh_l,
   from=frame(bone=thigh_l, toward=calf_l), toward=(down, forward=40))`, the same for the right, before the foot reaches;
   unsaid, the thighs turn with the pelvis and the knees point at the door), the hips and chest turned toward it
   (`aim(pelvis, ...)`, `aim($Trunk, ..., toward=(forward, left=35, up=5))`), the free arm hanging a little out (`aim(upperarm_r, ..., toward=(down, right=15, forward=10))`),
   each foot held on its spot by two points -- `reach(heel_l, to=point(bone=foot_l, relative=character))` and
   `reach(stand_l, to=point(bone=ball_l, relative=character))` -- and LAST the palm's POINT onto the handle's point
   (`point(socket=palm_grip_l)` to `point(socket=$Door.Knob, at=(...))`): the hand keeps its grip and the arm carries it.
   *Pointer (2026-11-12): the body typed above is the hand route. A `reach` with `[share=]` turns the trunk toward its place and the free arm counters it, with no `aim(pelvis)` or `aim($Trunk)` line ([The body joins a reach](HUMAN-GRASP-AND-REACH.md#the-body-joins-a-reach)). Try the share on the palm's reach first and type a body line only to override it. Not tried on this door key: whether the share's turn replaces its hips, knees and feet lines is not shown (its feet are held by reaches, not plants).*
   **A knob at 91 cm is at wrist height:** with the shoulder held still the hand reaches only about 30 cm round it, so an
   arm alone opens a door about 25 degrees and the person stands stiff while it does (the fixture's first cut). A fresh
   FRAME grip follows the handle's axis round behind the person (on the fixture: past the shoulder's twist stop).
   Let the head only glance at the handle (`aim(neck, ..., weight=0.3)`): the neck chain starts at the upper chest, and
   a full look down at a handle at hip height hunches the person over it.
5. **The person's clip:** `$KeyGrip@$ToGrip`, `$KeyOpen@$ToOpen`, and
   `reach(limb_l, from=$PalmAt, to=$KnobEnd, weight=$Hold, drive=$Door)` weighing fully from the grip to the release;
   the same four foot reaches as the key, the whole clip (a `plant()` by the ball alone lets the knee swing and turn the
   thigh into the hanging hand); **the reach weighing in WITH the approach and out with the way home** (a partly weighted
   reach draws its goal along the line to the handle and the limb solve keeps the elbow clear of the body; on the keys'
   blend alone, a grip from above swung the forearm through the belly), and key two's elbow aim held in the clip for the
   whole hold (`aim(upperarm_l, ..., weight=$Hold)` before the reach); and **both keys easing out while the person goes home** -- a rest pose that carries no
   hips or legs leaves a key at full weight holding the crouch after the release. The build says how far the door went
   (*"moves along its clip from frame 0.0 to 26.6 of 60 (44.4 degrees at the furthest)"*) and how far the reach had to
   carry the hand (under 7.5 cm on the fixture). A look or a second hold on the door rides it.
   **Closing is the same reach played back:** ease the open key out while the reach still holds the handle -- the hand
   goes back along the handle's arc and the door follows it to its stop. A round knob is turned before the pull (a key of
   the closed hand turned about the knob's axis, the way the forearm can still turn) and back as the door latches; hold
   it turned only until the latch clears, then let the pull key take the wrist back (held turned through the pull, the
   fixture's forearm went into the shirt). **Keep the pulling arm's elbow soft and say where it goes:** an arm laid out
   straight in a key has no elbow direction, and between keys the solve's choice can flip sides in one frame; bring the
   body nearer instead, and put an aim on the upper arm (`aim(upperarm_l, ..., toward=(down, left=30, forward=15),
   relative=spine_05)`) before the reach so the elbow is carried out from the body.
6. **How far a door opens from where the person stands is the body's.** On the fixture: the arm alone, 25 degrees; the
   body sitting into the pull, 44 degrees. Wider is a step back, which is the feet's (not built).
7. **The stage and the camera:** stand the person beside the door's swing, not in it -- the build checks it: a part a reach
   drives is in the final collision check where it stands at every frame and between frames, and only the hand that holds
   it passes through it (its note: "checked where it stands at each of its N frames ... the hand that holds it ('hand_l'
   and the 107 bones below it) is not stopped by it"); render the door at 30 degrees
   before writing the motion. Look from where the OPENING shows: on the pull side, behind the person and a little to
   the latch side (about 105 degrees round on the fixture), where the leaf stands angled open beside the dark doorway
   and the arm reaches out to the handle. From the hinge side the leaf covers the person mid-pull; from straight
   across the leaf faces the camera and hides the gap.
8. **The Blueprint:** the build writes `GC_Swing_<Part>` (degrees, `GC_Shift_<Part>` in cm for a slide) on the
   person's clip. Each Tick, `GetAnimInstance(Body).GetCurveValue("GC_Swing_Door")` into `MakeRotator(Yaw=...)` into
   `Door.K2_SetRelativeRotation`. For a simulated render, BeginPlay turns `SetUpdateAnimationInEditor` back on for
   the body and face.
9. **Look close up at the first touch.** A flush, handle-less door has no edge a finger can get behind while it is
   shut; such kitchens use push-to-open latches. That is unresolved for the counter fixture (an owner's call); a
   door with a knob or a handle has none of it.

### A drawer the hand pulls (built 2026-10-25)

The same method as the door, with a part that SLIDES. Fixture: `Content/GraphCodeExamples/HouseDrawer_20261025/` -- a chest
of five drawers (`HouseDrawer.gcshape`; brief and sizes in `Docs/Design/Assets/HouseDrawer/BUILD-GUIDE.md`),
`HouseDrawerSlide.gcmove` (the drawer's travel), `HouseDrawerKeysHover.gcmove`, `HouseDrawerKeys.gcmove`,
`HouseDrawerKeysOpen.gcmove` (the keys), `HouseDrawerPull.gcmove` (the person) and `BP_HouseDrawerPlayback`.

1. **The drawer's file:** `Slide = clip(frames=40) { shift(Drawer, axis=Y, by=$Out) }` with `Out = profile(0=0, 1=-40)
   [linear]` -- a centimetre a frame, so a frame of the clip is a distance out (a full-extension slide runs its own length).
   Build the drawer on a socket at its face (`[pivot=Runner]`, the frame's own axes) so its own Y is the slide.
2. **What the hand touches is the drawer's collision:** the front slab, the box behind it, and the pull's BAR alone (not its
   posts), three convex pieces that do not touch, so the room behind the bar stays open for the fingers.
3. **Stand him so the drawer, out, clears him:** a top drawer of a standard 48 in chest puts the pull at about 111 cm, a
   standing reach; on a 32 in chest it sits at about 69 cm, below the hanging palm, and the drawer comes out into the
   thighs. The pull in front of the hand's own side (on the fixture the chest is centred 15 cm to his right, its face 45 cm
   out). The build's final check carries the drawer, so a pull that brings it into him is refused.
4. **A thin bar is taken by a hand that arrives hooked.** A 12 mm bar leaves 2.3 cm behind it: a key that starts with the
   hand ON the bar is refused at every finger curl ("form fitting needs a legal starting pose", a different finger each
   time), because fingers differ in length. Build a HOVER key (the hand hooked -- the middle joints bent 45, the little
   finger less -- 2.5 cm above the bar and 1 cm back, no grab), then the grip key from it: `pose(source=<hover>,
   from=reference)`, the reach weighing in over the first half (`Arrive = profile(0=0, 0.5=1)`) and
   `grab(fist_r, ...)` after it. The bar lies under the FIRST FINGER BONES, not the palm: on this body about 4.5 cm along
   the fingers from `palm_grip_r` (read with a `[readings=(0)]` dry run). The thumb in the grip (`fist_r`): left out and
   spread, it pointed into his waist as the drawer came.
5. **The pull key:** the drawer at a time of its clip (`at=frame(25)`, 25 cm), the hips a little back and down, the knees
   forward over the toes, the elbow drawn back beside him, and LAST the hand's point on the bar's axis
   (`point(socket=palm_grip_r, at=(...))` to `point(socket=$Drawer.Pull)`).
6. **The clip:** as the door's -- one `reach(..., drive=$Drawer)` holds the pull out and back in, the open key eased out
   pushes it home; the little finger opens a beat after the others (together, its tip passed through the ring finger's).
   The build: *"$Drawer moves along its clip from frame 0.0 to 25.0 of 40 (25.0 cm at the furthest) ... the hand is carried
   at most 1.70 cm from where the handle can be"*.
7. **The Blueprint:** `GC_Shift_Drawer` (cm along the part's own axis): each Tick, `MakeVector(X, Y + value, Z)` of the
   drawer's rest place into `Drawer.K2_SetRelativeLocation`.

**The arm carrier is the opening.** While the reach weighs fully, the door stands at the frame of its clip whose
handle is nearest where the FK pose carries the palm. So measure the carrier: a copy of the file with the reach's
`weight=0` and `[readings=(...)]` on the build line gives the palm per key, in a dry run. Start the palm on the
shut handle's bearing from the hinge (or the door waits shut, then whips), and end it on the handle's arc. Keep
the arm bent at full opening: a straight arm at its reach flips its elbow.

Prepare joint limits and sign checks through [RIG.md](RIG.md) before copying anatomical angles into motion.
Keep source-specific reference lines distinct from local rig axes. Do not use an undefined phone neck angle as a
`swing` value. For a phone or keyboard posture, inspect the whole person and the display together, not just a hand.

Render the approach, first touch, maximum door opening, passage and release from side and overhead views. Play the
coordinated hand and door motion. For devices, inspect palm, thumb, working fingers, wrist, neck, display and foot
support through task entry, use and exit. Check for a slipping handle, a floating phone, bent wrists, unreachable
keys and a screen outside the intended view. These are authoring checks; the only built fixture here is the door the hand opens.

## What is not known yet

- Door push/pull trunk posture, foot placement and arm position were not found. Neither was the hand-to-handle
  timing. No phase durations, opening trajectories or measured hand force curves are supplied here.
- The retained door dimensions lack some section identifiers and approach conditions. They do not specify an
  entire compliant door layout. Their missing conditions must not become animation posture assumptions.
- Phone viewing distance, holding wrist angle and thumb posture were not found. Phone height, tap trajectories
  and action timing cannot be recovered from the reported head angles.
- Several phone head, neck and gaze angle definitions were not retained. Their values stay side by side; no
  conversion, sign or joint mapping is known here.
- Measured typing wrist extension, shoulder abduction and neck flexion were not found. The workstation rows remain
  setup guidance. Keypress phases and typing cadence were not established.
- No automatic door interaction, attention selection, ergonomic setup solver or centre-of-mass/balance verb is
  documented in the authoring guides. Native contact, object playback and runtime interaction need their actual
  setup and verification.
