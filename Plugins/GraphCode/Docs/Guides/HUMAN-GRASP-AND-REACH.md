# Human movement: grasping and reaching

This guide says what a hand does when it takes hold of something, and what an arm does on the way there. It gives the
numbers a result can be checked against. How a grip is built and fitted in GraphCode is [HANDS.md](HANDS.md) and
[POSES.md](POSES.md); this guide points into them and does not repeat them.

**What the numbers are.** Each row names its source and what was read. *Table* means the value came from a table in the
full text. *Abstract* means only the abstract was read. *Secondary* means a page quoted another author. *Derived* means
arithmetic on cited rows, and the arithmetic is shown. Populations are given where the source gives them. Values from
one lab or a few people are examples, not norms. Copy a whole consistent set from one row. Never mix extremes from
different rows into one pose.

## What a correct-looking result is

- **A reach** opens the hand as it travels. The hand is widest in the second half of the reach, then closes on the
  object. At its widest it is about 4 cm wider than the object (Furmanek 2019), not exactly as wide as it.
- **The hand slows for longer than it speeds up.** About two thirds of the reach is slowing down.
- **A grip fits the object.** A thin cylinder bends the fingers a lot. A wide one bends them a little. The outer
  finger joint (DIP) bends less than the middle joint (PIP): far less on a thin cylinder (ratio 0.58 at 10 mm), nearly
  as much on a wide one (0.88 at 120 mm).
- **The wrist is bent back (extended) when a hand grips.** A grip with the wrist dead straight looks stiff.
- **A far reach brings the trunk in.** The trunk leans and turns when the arm alone cannot get there.

## The movement, in order

### A reach-to-grasp

Times are shares of the whole reach ("movement time"). The sources disagree a little on each, so ranges are shown.

| Event | When | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hand starts to move | 0% | |
| Peak hand speed | **31.5-34.0%** of movement time (table cell 32.0 ± 5.4%). So about 2/3 of the reach is slowing down | Furmanek et al. 2019 (table), n = 13 |
| Peak hand speed, review statement | In the first half of the movement | Broome et al. 2019 |
| Maximum grip aperture (MGA, the widest the thumb and index finger get) | **64.4-69.0%** of movement time (table cell 66.4 ± 6.2%) | Furmanek 2019 (table) |
| MGA, review | Intercept at **60%** of movement time, rising **1.8% per cm** of object size (32 experiments). Always in the second half, and later for larger objects | Smeets & Brenner 1999 |
| MGA, grasp lasting 400 ms or 800 ms | **61%** at 400 ms, **75%** at 800 ms (self-paced). Jeannerod 1984 put self-paced MGA at about 75% | Heath et al. 2012, n = 15 (11 M), aged 20-28, objects 20-50 mm |
| Peak opening, then peak deceleration | Both in the second half | Broome et al. 2019 |
| The grip closes | The digits curve strongly near the object. Each digit approaches its contact point roughly perpendicular to the surface | Smeets & Brenner 1999 |
| Whole reach-to-grasp, 24-36 cm | **904-1122 ms** (table cell 904.3 ± 95.0) | Furmanek 2019 (table), n = 13 |

At 30 frames a second, 0.904-1.122 s is about 27-34 frames (*derived*: frames = seconds x fps, as in
[ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#compose-movement-in-stages)).

Speed and vision change the picture:

- Less vision (peripheral vision, eyes closed, touch only) gives a larger, earlier MGA and usually a longer movement
  (Smeets & Brenner 1999, review of 4 studies).
- Moving as fast as possible gives a **15% larger MGA** (Wing 1986, quoted by Smeets & Brenner 1999).
- The grip prepares about **150 ms before** an expected impact (Johansson & Westling 1988, abstract).

### Grasp types

| Item | What the hand does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Power grip | The object is clamped between the flexed fingers and the palm. The thumb lies roughly in the plane of the palm and pushes back | Napier 1956 (abstract) |
| Precision grip | The object is pinched between the finger pads and the opposing thumb pad | Napier 1956 (abstract) |
| Pad opposition | Force runs parallel to the palm, pad against pad (a needle, a small ball). The thumb is virtual finger 1 | Feix et al. 2016 |

One more set, a different method and sample:

| Value | Population | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mean flexion: PIP 43°, MCP 28°, DIP 26° (unclear whether per cylinder size or averaged over all sizes) | n = 48 (11 F, 37 M), 5 cylinder sizes, sensor glove | Gülke et al. 2010 (abstract) |

### How wide the hand opens

MGA is the maximum thumb-to-index distance. MGA = maximum grip aperture.

| Value | Population and convention | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MGA grows with object size at a slope of **0.82** (mean of 35 published experiments). The model predicts 0.81 | Review of experiments | Smeets & Brenner 1999 |
| Objects **7.2 / 5.4 / 3.6 cm** give MGA of about **11.1 / 9.5 / 7.8 cm** | n = 13 (2 F), 23.9 ± 6.8 y, reaches of 24, 30 and 36 cm, real objects | Furmanek 2019 (table 2) |
| From those numbers MGA is about **3.9-4.2 cm larger** than the object, with a slope of about **0.92** | *Derived* from the 30 cm values of the row above | |
| MGA is always a little larger than the object, and comes later for larger objects | n = 12 young (mean 27.7 y), cylinders 5, 7 and 9 cm | Holt et al. 2013 |
| Most grasps are used on objects under 500 g, and most need a hand opening of 5 cm or less | Grasp survey | Feix 2016 |

**Where the sources disagree.** The slope is 0.82 (35 experiments) against about 0.92 (Furmanek). The excess over the
object is "slightly larger" in Holt but about 4 cm in Furmanek. The size depends on marker placement. When MGA happens
is 60% plus 1.8% per cm (review), 64-69% (Furmanek), or 61% for 400 ms against 75% for 800 ms (Heath). Speed effects
also disagree: Wing 1986 found faster movements gave a later MGA, while Wallace & Weeks and Heath found earlier.

### The wrist in a power grip

Degrees from neutral.

| Value | Population | Source |
|---|---|---|

### The trunk joins the reach

| Value | Population | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The trunk joins in mainly when arm plus shoulder-blade motion cannot reach the target. It starts with the arm and keeps moving until contact. Trunk flexion finishes last | n = 6, 5 targets (2 within arm's length, 3 beyond) | Kaminski, Bock & Gentile 1995 (abstract) |
| When the trunk movement is instructed, it starts about **175 ms before** the hand and ends about **200 ms after** it | Fast planar reaches with forward or backward trunk motion | Ma & Feldman 1995 (abstract) |

**Where the sources disagree.** The trunk starts about 175 ms early when the trunk movement is instructed (Ma &
Feldman) but at the same time as the hand in natural reaches (Kaminski, Rossi). It adds hand travel only after peak
hand speed (Rossi).

## The most common mistakes

Read from the rows above.

1. **The same finger angles whatever the object.** The index PIP goes from 105.5° on a 10 mm cylinder to 24.2° on a
   120 mm one. A fixed fist angle is wrong for both.
2. **A hand opened exactly to the object's width.** The widest opening is a few centimetres larger than the object
   (about 3.9-4.2 cm in Furmanek 2019).
3. **The hand widest at the start or at contact.** The widest opening comes in the second half, at about 60-69% of the
   reach in the studies that measured it (75% in a slow one), and later for larger objects.
4. **A symmetrical, minimum-jerk speed curve.** A real reach peaks at about 32% of its duration and then slows for the
   rest. Use a skewed profile with a long final slowdown.
5. **The fingertip joint closing first.** The knuckle (MCP) or the middle joint (PIP) starts first. The DIP moves last.
6. **A dead-straight wrist while gripping.** People grip with the wrist extended: 35° in one study, with grip strength
   falling at 15° of extension; 15° gave the strongest grip in another. Do not hold the wrist at 0° and call it a grip.

## How to author it in GraphCode

[HANDS.md](HANDS.md) and [POSES.md](POSES.md) own the mechanics. This is only what to take from the tables.

| You want | Where it is built | What the numbers do |
|---|---|---|
| A fist | [HANDS.md Step 3](HANDS.md#step-3-make-a-fist), [POSES.md](POSES.md#close-a-hand-grips-and-fists-that-look-right) | Check the stopped finger angles against the cylinder table. A fist is a very thin cylinder |
| A hand closed on a handle | [HANDS.md Step 5](HANDS.md#step-5-hold-a-handle-a-sword-a-hammer) | The handle's thickness decides the angles. Ask each joint for nearly its limit and let the prop stop it, then compare the stopped angles with the table |
| Fingers close in a real order | `MCP = bones(index_01_r, ...)`, `PIP`, `DIP` as in HANDS Step 3 | Knuckle first, then middle joint, then tip. A collision pose does this by itself ([POSES.md](POSES.md)) |
| The thumb | HANDS Step 3 and Step 7: out, roll, curl, across | The notes give no thumb angles in a cylinder grip. See "What is not known yet" |
| A pinch (pad to pad) | No recipe in the guides | The nearest tools are `contact(...)` with a thumb pad on the fingers and `reach(index_r, contact=IndexPad, ...)`. Nobody has built a pinch recipe here |
| An arm reach | `reach(arm_R, to=$Point)` ([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#what-each-implemented-instruction-does)); a reach on a whole body roots the arm chain at the upper arm ([HANDS.md Step 2](HANDS.md#step-2-rig-the-hand-gcrig)) | Check the target's distance against the reach table. The reach note says how many cm short it ended. A far place or a cut: `[share=]` on the reach brings the trunk and hips ([The body joins a reach](#the-body-joins-a-reach)) |
| The hand opening as it travels | `swing` on the finger bones with a `profile` | Peak opening at about 60-69% of the reach, about 4 cm wider than the object |
| A reach's speed shape | The timing of your key poses (below), not the reach's `weight=` | A weight ramp from the start hands the whole journey to IK; see "Key poses carry the arm" |

Convert a reach time to frames: frames = seconds x fps. A 1 s reach at `fps:30` is 30 frames.

### Key poses carry the arm; IK lands the last few centimetres

This is how an animator builds a reach, and it is the way to build one here. **Contact is the last mile, never the
route.** If a reach's `weight=` rises from 0 at the start of the clip, the IK solver picks the whole path from the
hanging arm to the object: it tucks the elbow in against the body, and on the way the forearm sweeps across the belly or
through the shirt. Nothing you wrote said that, and no setting fixes it. Raising a collision `gives=` only lets the
solver tuck the arm in further.

1. **Pick the beats** from the table above: the start, the peak speed (about a third of the way), the widest hand
   opening (about two thirds), the approach just short of the object, and contact.
2. **Solve each contact pose once, on its own.** Build it in a keys `.gcmove` with the reach at full weight from its
   first frame (`weight=` a profile that is 1 throughout), the arm already at the object, the fingers closing on it.
   Author where the elbow goes with the arm's own swings beside the reach (for a cup close in front of the belly, the
   elbow out to the side, as a person lifts it). Render the pose and read its notes before anything uses it.
3. **Block the travel in a timeline.** Read each key pose with `pose(source=)` and enter it with `from=reference` at its
   beat, so the arm moves between keys in joint space, on the path you chose. Add in-between keys where the path needs
   them: if the forearm passes close to the body, a key with the elbow out keeps it clear.
4. **Let the reach land only the contact.** Its `weight=` stays 0 through the travel and rises to 1 over the last few
   frames before contact, so the solver corrects centimetres, not the route.
5. **Check the whole motion, not the end pose.** A refusal mid-travel is a path you authored too close; fix the key,
   not a tolerance.

The kitchen cup fixture is built this way: `KitchenCupKeys.gcmove` solves the stand and the grip once,
`KitchenCup.gcmove` travels between them.

**A pose that places a HELD thing** (where a sword, a tool or a gun ends a motion) is solved the same way, with the
thing as the target: `reach(chain, from=$Grip, to=$Place)` puts its grip there and `aim(chain, from=$Axis, at=$Point)`
points its length (a `frame()` on the hand's grip socket, `toward=` the part's long axis); the wrist then follows from
the aim instead of from angles you guessed. The chain must end at a bone that carries the bone the thing rides: on a
MetaHuman, `arm_r` ends at `middle_metacarpal_r`, which does not carry `palm_r`, so declare one ending at `hand_r` in
the character's `.gcrig`, never at the unlimited carrier itself
([HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md](HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md#a-cut-with-a-held-blade)).

### Tune a key pose in seconds, not minutes

Tune the arm of a contact pose as a **one-frame clip**, before any fingers close:

```gcmove
ArmKey = clip(frames=1) {
  swing(upperarm_r, axis=fold, by=30)      # the arm you are tuning
  swing(upperarm_r, axis=side, by=-30)
  swing(lowerarm_r, axis=fold, by=60)
  reach(limb_r, from=$Palm, to=$Grasp, weight=$Held)
}
```

Dry-run it with `gc_apply` (`dry_run:true`). On a clothed MetaHuman with `worn:` this took about 14 s a round (measured
2026-10-12). The same key as a 30-frame clip with the fingers closing under `collision=` took about 3.5 minutes: the
collision fit runs over every frame. So change one or two numbers a round on the one-frame arm, and add the fingers only
once the palm lands where you want it.

**Find the place before you dry-run it: the bubble.** `gc_render` draws every place the arm can hold on the key's source pose
(bright: every joint in range; the body and the key's build are tried on the points you ask); the call is in
[HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md](HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md#a-cut-with-a-held-blade), step 2 (leave out `aim:` and the blade bone when the
hand holds nothing). Give it `key:"<your .gcmove's path>:<the key's clip>"`: the trunk then turns and the hips rise or sink as that
key's build does, and asking a point builds that key with this arm's reach moved there, nothing saved. Its `build` answer is the
build's own ("accepted", or why not) and the one to trust: the build's balance moves the shoulder after the picture is drawn. A
two-handed key works too: the bubble's arm takes its own reach, and the other hand stays where the key puts it.

**The place is the bubble's MAX EXTENSION, every time** (user-ruled 2026-10-23: *"always use the maximum bubble approach, or else the
hand look weird"*). Ask toward the target with no `distance` and write the max line it answers: `point(at=...)` for an empty hand,
reached with the hand itself (`reach(arm3_l, to=$Place)`), the hand as the forearm carries it there. Do not pull the place a few
centimetres inside the max to bend the elbow, and do not add a palm facing (`toward=`) the bubble did not test: BlueEdge's wall switch,
placed 13 cm inside its max with the palm turned to the wall by hand, built with the wrist bent back 50 deg and the forearm twisted to
its stop, and the hand looked awkward.

Read the build's notes each round and fix the largest problem first:

| The note says | What it means | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| "its joints asked N degrees past their ranges (... wrist N)" | The hand cannot face the target from this arm | Turn the forearm in the arm pose, or take the object from another side (a round cup can be held anywhere round it) |
| "could not be put where the file asked - N cm short ... Which way: ... sideways" | The arm cannot reach that point in that plane | Move the elbow out or in, lean the trunk toward the object, or step closer |
| "could not establish incoming body-clearance baseline: 'A' intersects 'B'" | Your own arm pose is already inside the body | Fix the arm pose: it is your path, not the reach |
| "maximum orientation residual N degrees" | The palm lands turned away by that much | As for the wrist row |

Render the key from the front and the side before a timeline uses it ([HANDS.md](HANDS.md#check-it)).

**The wrist.** The guides do not map a wrist control. On the hand rig in HANDS.md the wrist bone is rigid. On a body
rig, probe the wrist's axis and sign with a two-frame clip and a render, the way
[WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md#2-draw-the-legs-in-place) tests the shoulder, before you write an extension angle.

arm still ends short.

**Look at the result.** Close-ups from the palm side, the back of the hand and the far side of the object
([HANDS.md](HANDS.md#check-it)). A grip that passes every check can still look wrong.

### The body joins a reach

*User, 2026-10-15: "You declare you intent, solver do the rest. Never guess where hips should go, where chest should go,
etc."* Built the same day (MOVE-ACTION1, `Docs/GC-MOVE-ACTION-INTENT-PLAN.md`). The worked key is clip `C0` of
`Content/__MCPTests/SwingCheck_20261022/SwingCheckKeys.gcmove` (built 2026-10-22; `Blade`, `Face`, `Opponent`, `Sword`,
`Floor` are defined there): where the blade ends and points, `[share=]`, the plants, `balance`, the gaze.

```gcmove
CutLoad = frame(at=(up=149, forward=43, right=39), toward=(up, back=30))   # where the blade ends, which way it points
C0 = clip(source=/Game/__MCPTests/BlueEdgeProbe/A_Motion_Low1, frames=1) {
  reach(sword3_r, from=$Blade, to=$CutLoad) [share=0.4]    # the intent; the share is how much the body joins
  plant(stand_r, on=$Floor)  plant(stand_l, on=$Floor)    # the feet stay
  balance(pelvis, over=(stand_r, stand_l), max=35)        # the weight stays over them
  aim(neck, from=$Face, at=$Opponent)                     # the gaze
  collision(self, $Sword)
}
```

What the share (`0 < s <= 1`) does, read from the code and the help text; the build's notes say each step:
- **The trunk turns toward the place by the share**: from the first bone above the planted legs' hips up to the chest
  (`spine_01` to `spine_05` on a MetaHuman), each joint by the room it has. A place above the chest opens the chest upright
  toward it (a turn, not a tilt); for a place below, the trunk keeps its coil and takes the least turn onto it, which
  inclines it toward a low place.
- **A place that rides the pelvis or a leg is turned toward by the same rule as a place fixed in the room.** The hand's place goes with the bone, and the trunk takes the share's turn toward where the place is in each pose. A place the trunk itself carries (on a spine bone or what hangs from it), a socket's place, a place riding a prop, and a reach that goes OUT a way keep the behaviour they had.
- **The pelvis is not turned by it** when planted legs hang from it (note: `'pelvis' carries planted legs`): the hips are the
  plants' and balance's. No word turns the hips with the reach yet; that stays your line (the hand route in
  [HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md](HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md#a-cut-with-a-held-blade)).
- **The hips rise or sink for a place past the limb's reach.** With a `balance` line in the clip, a place beyond 0.95 of the
  limb's span moves the hips straight up or down by the excess, the planted legs re-solving with the feet staying; it stops
  at `balance(max=)` and where a leg would pass 0.97 of its span. A place farther ACROSS the floor than the limb spans is
  not brought in.
- **The free arm counters the working hand.** The rig's mirror chain (`hand_r` to `hand_l`) reaches a point away from the
  working hand through the chest, between waist and chest height, on its own side, riding the chest, at the reach's weight
  times the share. Above the chest it is drawn toward a waist chamber. With the working hand at or below the chest, the
  PUBLISHED build keeps it only half the shoulder's width out from the trunk's middle, which can put a low cut's free hand
  4 cm into the ribs (the build warns `keeps 'hand_l' in 'spine_03'`); write the free arm's line yourself there. (Fixed in the
  source 2026-11-12, MOVE-CUT1 B1, to the shoulder's width and 4 cm out; not in the published build yet.) A
  written `reach`, `aim` or other constraint line on a chain sharing a bone with that arm replaces it, and dropping the share
  switches it off (the code counts constraint lines, not plain `swing` lines; read from the code, not from a run).
- **The edge is the arm's when `up=` is omitted.** A body-words `frame(toward=...)` with no `up=`, reached with `from=`, asks
  only where the blade points; its edge is the line from the shoulder to the place made square to the blade, then turned to
  the forearm the solve built (up to 8 passes). Keys each solved alone can still come far apart (85 degrees in the swing
  check), so for a swing name the edge at both keys ([the swing's keys](HUMAN-THROW-AND-STRIKE.md#a-cut-with-a-held-blade)).
- **Cautions.** A share cannot make a push lean and can undo a lean you wrote ([HUMAN-LIFT-CARRY-PUSH.md](HUMAN-LIFT-CARRY-PUSH.md),
  measured 2026-10-23). A written pelvis, chest, thigh, knee, heel, free-arm or edge line is the plan's OVERRIDE (or your style):
  read the notes for how it combined with the share; the guides do not show every combination.

## What is not known yet

- **No thumb angles in a cylinder grip.** Shimawaki left the thumb out because its position "varies greatly among
  individuals". No other source that was read gave them.
- **Cutkosky's 1989 grasp tree** was named in the research but not read. Its details are not in this guide.
- **Grasp names for most of the 33 types** are not confirmed. Only the 16 listed in "Grasp types" were confirmed in
  the Feix text. The names of #25 and #8 in the frequency table are unknown.
- **Numeric grip-force safety margins** (how many percent above slipping) were not found.
- **Wing & Fraser 1983** (the thumb's path is straighter than the index finger's) was named but not read.
- **Barnes work-area radii** (the usual figures are roughly 35-40 cm and 55-65 cm), a **comfortable reach as a fraction
  of arm length**, and "the trunk starts at about 90% of arm length" were not found in any source that was read.
- **Fitts' law** and natural pointing durations of 0.5-1.0 s over 20-40 cm were not confirmed. Only the reach-to-grasp
  durations (Furmanek) are.
- **Jeannerod's own values** were reached only through Heath's citation (about 75%).
- **How a reach `weight=` profile maps to hand speed** is not stated in the guides. Check the hand's speed in playback
  against the 32% peak. Do not assume a weight ramp gives a bell-shaped speed curve.
- ~~**No automatic trunk recruitment** is shown in the guides.~~ (SUPERSEDED 2026-10-15: a reach with `[share=]` turns the
  trunk by the share you write and rises the hips for a place past the limb's span, see
  [The body joins a reach](#the-body-joins-a-reach). It is not the studies' rule of WHEN the trunk joins by distance: the
  turn is the share's amount, and the hips' rise starts only past 0.95 of the limb's span.)
- **Eye-hand timing** (where the eyes go before and during a reach) is in [HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md](HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md).
