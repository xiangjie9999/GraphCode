# Human movement: sit to stand, stand to sit, crouch, kneel and the floor

[Human movement guides](HUMAN-MOVEMENT.md) · this page: how a person sits, stands, crouches, kneels and gets up from the floor.

This guide says how a person gets up from a chair and sits down again, how deep a squat or a kneel goes, and how a
person gets up off the floor. It gives phases, durations and joint angles. The GraphCode guides have no sit, squat or
floor-rise recipe. This guide says which existing guide supplies each piece and where the gaps are.

**What the numbers are.** The sources are laboratory studies, mostly young healthy adults. Older-adult rows say so.
The squat and kneel numbers come from joint-replacement research. The floor-rise numbers come from older adults. Each
row ends with a source key in square brackets; the keys are listed under "Sources". Labels:

- **C**: cited, full text read.
- **Ca**: cited, abstract only.
- **Cs**: cited through a named secondary page.
- **D**: derived here by arithmetic, which is shown.

Seat height, foot position and arm position change the result, so each row gives them. Movement start and end are
defined differently in each paper, so percentages of the movement are not interchangeable between rows. Copy a whole
consistent set from one row. Never mix values from different studies into one pose.

## What a correct-looking result is

- **Rising from a chair starts with the trunk, not the legs.** The trunk leans forward first. The buttocks leave the
  seat only after that. Then the body rises to upright and settles.
- **The lean depends on the seat.** In the one study that varied it, the maximum trunk lean was about 39° at a low seat
  (10-30 cm) and about 22° at a 60 cm seat.
- **Sitting down is not the rise played backwards.** It is slower and is controlled by the leg muscles lengthening.
- **A person who is old or weak uses the arms.** Older men used their arms to push off in the study that measured it.
- **Getting up from the floor goes through stages.** Supine to sit, to a kneel or all fours or a half-kneel, to a
  crouch, to standing. Nobody pops up in one smooth motion. It takes about 4 s or more.
- **A deep squat with the heels down needs a lot of ankle.** About 38.5° of dorsiflexion was required ([KM15]).

## The movement, in order

### Rising from a chair: four phases

Schenkman et al. 1990, nine healthy individuals, controlled conditions (abstract) [SC90].

| Phase | Boundaries and what happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I flexion-momentum | Forward trunk flexion that generates the initial momentum for rising. Ends as the person leaves the seat | [SC90] Ca |
| II momentum-transfer | From leaving the seat to maximal ankle dorsiflexion. Forward momentum of the upper body becomes forward and upward momentum of the whole body | [SC90] Ca |
| III extension | The body rises to full upright | [SC90] Ca |
| IV stabilization | A stabilization phase (the abstract gives no further detail) | [SC90] Ca |

Timing of the same phase scheme and related events:

| Study (population, set-up) | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Li 2021: 30 healthy men, 25.7 ± 2.01 years, 43 cm backless seat, feet shoulder width, arms folded on chest | Whole movement **1.60 s** (T). Phase 1 **0%-18% T**, ending at hip lift-off. Phase 2 **18%-40% T**, ending at maximal ankle dorsiflexion (T2 = 40% T). Phase 3 **40%-91% T**. Phase 4 **91% T** to the end. The trunk stops flexing forward at **44% T**, 4% T after peak ankle dorsiflexion (the authors' "transition period") | [LI21] C |
| Li 2021, centre of gravity travel per phase | Phase 1: **51.41 mm** forward, almost no rise. Phase 2: **109.35 mm** forward, **74.21 mm** up. Phase 3: **40.44 mm** forward, **168.85 mm** up | [LI21] C |
| Krafft 2022: 20 able-bodied adults (10 women 23.9 ± 3.4 years, 10 men 27.9 ± 5.8), seat at knee height, unassisted, normal floor | Seat-off at **43.4 ± 4.4%** of the movement. Movement start and end were defined from centre-of-mass velocity and height, seat-off from force sensors in the seat | [KR22] C |
| Mamizu 2026: 23 healthy men, 26.4 ± 2.8 years, seat height matched to the lower limb with the thigh midpoint at the seat's front edge, arms relaxed at the sides | Maximum hip flexion at **42.7 ± 3.23%** of the movement (the 41%-50% interval was their transition zone). Trunk flexion (relative to the pelvis) preceded hip flexion and reached its maximum before the hip did | [MA26], [MA26A] C for the percentages, Ca for the order |
| Zheng 2026 (pelvic angles over the cycle) | Pelvic tilt angle falls sharply to a trough within **44.48%-53.19%** of the cycle | [MA26A] Ca |

**Where the sources disagree.** Seat-off (the end of the flexion phase) is at **18%** of the movement in [LI21] and at
**43.4%** in [KR22]. The start and end definitions differ, and the fetches do not reconcile them. Both are shown. Do
not average them.

| Tebbache and Hamaoui 2021: 14 healthy adults, 22.9 ± 3 years, thighs horizontal and feet flat, self-selected foot width, backrest 90° to 130° | 2,267 ms at a 90° backrest rising to 2,553 ms at 130°. Their "postural phase" 738 ms to 1,080 ms and "focal phase" 374-610 ms (phase definitions were not in the fetch) | not studied | [TE21] C |
| Kerr 1997: 50 normal subjects aged 20.1-78.3 (mean 46.8), sensor-based, six repeats at self-selected speed | mean 1.91 s | mean 1.97 s. Forward-lean velocity was greater in rising than in sitting down, recovery velocity greater in sitting down | [KE97] Ca |

*D:* in both studies that measured both directions, sitting down took longer than rising (1.97 s against 1.91 s;
1.43 s against 1.21 s). The note's summary says the rising means span 1.21-2.27 s. One row above (Tebbache, backrest at
130°) gives 2.553 s, which is outside that span. This guide's author noticed the mismatch; the note does not explain it.
Li cites a source saying that for people with dyskinesia rising is comfortable within 2 s and very difficult at 5 s,
and recommends that sit-to-stand assist devices not go below 1.6 s or above 5 s ([LI21] Cs).

Convert to frames with frames = seconds x fps ([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#compose-movement-in-stages)). A 1.6 s rise at
`fps:30` is 48 frames.

| Element | What was found | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Seat height | Review of 39 experimental studies: a higher seat lowered knee moments by up to 60% and hip moments by up to 50%. A lower seat increased the need for momentum or for repositioning the feet | [JA02] Ca |
| Seat height and trunk speed | 11 young (25-36 years) and 10 older (61-79 years) adults rose from four seat heights set relative to knee height: trunk flexion angular velocity increased as the seat dropped. ## The most common mistakes

All are *derived* from the rows above.

1. **The trunk upright until the buttocks leave the seat.** Every source has forward trunk flexion first, ending
   18%-44% of the way through the movement.
2. **The wrong lean for the seat.** A 10-30 cm seat with a lean near 22° (measured about 39°), or a 60 cm seat with a
   lean near 39° (measured about 22°).
3. **A rise outside the measured times, or a sit-down faster than the rise.** Chair-rise means in the table run
   1.21-2.55 s. Both studies that measured both directions found sitting down slower.
4. **Sitting down as a simple reverse of the rise.** The authors of the stand-to-sit study say it is controlled
   eccentrically and is not a simple reverse.
5. **An upright, one-motion rise from lying to standing with no intermediate support.** Every floor study lists a sit,
   kneel, half-kneel, all-fours or crouch stage. Also, a floor rise faster than about 4 s: the shortest times listed are
   4.1 s and 4.2 s.
6. **Both hands off the floor throughout, for an older or weak character.** Older and impaired adults kept hands and
   knees down.

## How to author it in GraphCode

There is no sit, stand, squat, kneel or floor-rise recipe in the guides. POSES.md says there is no built-in crouch
activity. These are the parts that exist.

| You want | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Legs bending as the hips drop, feet staying put | `plant(leg_R, on=$Floor, contact=Sole_R, weight=...)` plus `swing` on `thigh`, `calf`, `foot` | [ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#compose-movement-in-stages): prefer `plant()` on the floor with sole contacts, so the IK bends the knees as the hips drop |
| A squat deeper than the ankle allows | `shift(Root, ...)` to move the hips back and lean the trunk forward over them | ANIMATION.md: do not push the ankle past its range. A squat needs about 38.5° of ankle dorsiflexion ([KM15]); check the rig's `foot` range ([WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md#1-give-the-rig-foot-contacts-that-ride-their-own-bones)) |
| The trunk leaning first | `swing(spine_0N, axis=fold, by=...)` with a `profile` that rises before the legs extend | On a rig built like RifleMan a positive fold leans forward. Mapping: [WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md#2-draw-the-legs-in-place). Check the sign in a one-pose render |
| Phase timing | A `profile` with keys at 0, 0.18, 0.40, 0.91 and 1 | Pick one study's fractions and keep them together. Li's 18% / 40% / 91% belong to a 1.60 s rise |
| The pelvis moving forward and up | `shift()` on the bone that owns this displacement | The centre-of-gravity travel is for the whole body, not a value to copy onto the root. Root extraction/force-lock removes root Z; for relative body height in an in-place game clip, use the pelvis with Animation translation retargeting. Check the [runtime ownership guidance](ANIMATION.md#root-travel-and-visible-body-height-in-a-game) and played contacts |
| Hands pushing on a seat or thigh | `reach(arm_R, to=$Point)` to a body-attached point, `point(bone=..., at=(...))` ([POSES.md](POSES.md#hold-an-object-while-moving)), or `contact(arm_R, contact=Palm, on=self, ...)` ([MOVE-PHYSICS.md](MOVE-PHYSICS.md#place-a-palm-against-the-body)) | A palm on a thigh that lifts follows it. Not built for a seat |
| Lying down, a floor transfer | `clip(gravity=true, collision=$Floor)`, `contact(pelvis, on=$Floor, weight=$Acquire)`, `physics($Legs, weight=...)` ([MOVE-PHYSICS.md](MOVE-PHYSICS.md#choosing-a-supporting-body)) | That guide covers falling and changing the supporting body surface. It says a passive fall is not proof of a controlled roll, and that there are no built-in human strength values |
| A chair | No recipe | A seat height could be declared like a tread, `ground(z=...)` ([STAIRS.md](STAIRS.md)). Nobody has used that for the pelvis. Do not assume it works; render a side view over a box, as STAIRS.md does |

**What GraphCode does not give you here.** `balance()` keeps the mass over the planted soles
([MOVE-BALANCE.md](MOVE-BALANCE.md)), but it is static: the momentum of a rise is authored by hand and judged by eye,
and the forward weight shift is yours to time (`balance(..., weight=, at=)`). A character
who is old or frail needs its own authored arm push-off. The grammar does not infer it.

## What is not known yet

From the research note's own gaps:

- **Sit to stand:** no hip, knee and trunk angle time series across the whole movement for a plain healthy rise. No
  seat-off angle in absolute degrees for a deliberate natural rise from a full-text source other than [KN15]. No
  timing of the stand-to-sit phases. Schenkman's own numbers (the 1990 paper was read only as an abstract).
- **Squat, crouch, kneel:** no timing for going down into or coming up out of a squat, crouch or kneel. No half-kneel
  or crouch angles. No joint angles for floor rises of young adults (only their strategy and speed ranking). No angles
  for kneeling down in a sequence with hands. The squat and kneel peaks are static maxima, not paths.
- **Not in GraphCode:** no sit, squat, kneel or floor-rise recipe. A seat is not built. `balance()` keeps the weight over
  standing feet ([MOVE-BALANCE.md](MOVE-BALANCE.md)); it does not carry the weight onto a seat or the hands.
