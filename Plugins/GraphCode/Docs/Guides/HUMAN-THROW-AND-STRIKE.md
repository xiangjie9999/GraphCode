# Human movement: throwing and striking

[Human movement guides](HUMAN-MOVEMENT.md) · this page: how a person throws overarm and how a person punches.

This guide says how the body builds speed in an overarm throw and a punch: which part moves first, when each part
peaks, and the joint angles and speeds that were measured. The data are for the overarm baseball pitch and for
punches. There is no source here for other throws (underhand, sidearm, a javelin, a football) or for kicks.

GraphCode has no throw or strike verb. [ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md) owns staging a movement; this guide gives the
numbers to check a result against and says plainly where the tools stop.

**What the numbers are.** Each row names its population and what was read: the full text, a table, the results or an
abstract. *Cited* means the number was on the page that was read. *Derived* means arithmetic on cited rows, shown. In
the pitch rows "foot contact" is stride-foot contact and "release" is ball release. Most pitchers are male, from
adolescents to professionals. Angle conventions differ between papers. Where a row states one, it is given. Copy a
whole consistent set from one row. Never mix values from different studies into one pose.

## What a correct-looking result is

- **A strike reaches FULL EXTENSION at its contact (user-ruled 2026-10-25).** A punch is always a fully extended arm: at
  impact the elbow is straight, the fist at the end of the arm laid out from the shoulder. Anything short of that is not a
  punch. The same holds for a sword swing (the sword arm extended through the cut) and every other strike (a kick's knee
  straight at contact). Place the target where the extended limb ends, never the limb where a target happens to be (see
  "How to author it" below).
- **The body throws, not just the arm.** The pelvis turns first and further than the torso. Each segment starts as the
  one nearer the body reaches top speed.
- **At foot contact the hips and shoulders are turned apart.** That gap is about 50-57° in the high-school, college and
  Division I pitchers measured, and 22-31° in the adolescents of another study. By release it has closed to about 12°.
- **The arm is cocked far back, then whips forward in about 50 ms.**
- **The trunk leans forward at release.**
- **A punch uses the legs.** More knee bend at impact and more leg force went with more punch force.
- **The order is not a perfect chain.** None of 208 pitches was fully proximal-to-distal. Do not animate a
  textbook sequence where every peak follows the last.

## The movement, in order

### An overarm pitch

Foot contact is 0% and release is 100%. Times as a share of that interval are from Seroyer 2010 (a review table)
unless a row says otherwise. Foot contact to ball release is **0.145 s** (Seroyer 2010).

| Event | When (% of foot contact to release) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stride-foot contact | 0% | |
| Pelvis peak speed | **25-39%** (Seroyer 2010). **12 ± 11%**, 596°/s (Orishimo 2023) | cited (secondary, Table 1), Seroyer. cited (abstract), Orishimo |
| Upper-trunk peak speed | **51-52%** (Seroyer). **36 ± 11%**, 959°/s (Orishimo) | same |
| End of late cocking | before the arm's peak external rotation | cited (secondary, text), Seroyer |
| Peak external rotation | **81%** | cited (secondary, Table 1), Seroyer |
| Elbow-extension peak | **90-95%** | same |
| Forward-trunk-tilt peak | 93-96% | same |
| Ball release | 100% | |
| Shoulder internal-rotation peak | **102-104%** (3-4 ms after release) | same |

- The arm goes from about 175° of external rotation to about 100° of internal rotation in **42-58 ms** (Seroyer 2010,
  text).
- The pelvis rotates earlier and further than the torso. Each segment starts as the one nearer the body reaches top
  speed (Seroyer 2010, text).
- *Derived* gap from Orishimo, assuming Seroyer's 0.145 s interval: (36 - 12)% x 0.145 s is about **35 ms** between
  pelvis and trunk peaks. This is a calculation, not a reported value.
- Order of peak angular velocity, 208 pitches by 22 pitchers: **none** was fully proximal-to-distal. 14 different
  orders were seen. The most common was **pelvis, trunk, arm, hand, forearm** (Scarborough et al. 2020, abstract).
- Professionals rotate the trunk **later** in the pitch (Aguinaldo et al. 2007, 38 pitchers, abstract; no numbers).
- 16 of 17 position and timing parameters were the same across youth, high school, college and pro (Fleisig et al.
  1999, abstract, no angle values).

**Where the sources disagree.**

- **Pelvis and trunk peak timing.** Seroyer's review puts them at 25-39% and 51-52% of the interval. Orishimo measured
  12 ± 11% and 36 ± 11%. Both agree the pelvis comes first.
- **Peak shoulder external rotation.** 165-175° (Seroyer review) against 179 ± 10° (Giordano, markerless in-game).
- **Hip-shoulder separation at foot contact.** 50 ± 12° (Orishimo, high school and college), 57 ± 10° (Giordano, D-I),
  and 22-31° (Johnson, adolescents).
- **Lead knee at foot contact.** 134.0° with 180° straight (Tanaka, about 46° flexion) against 52.2° (Giordano, the
  convention not stated). They are close if Giordano's is flexion. The note does not say so; this guide's author noticed
  it.
- **Textbook order against data.** Scarborough 2020 found no fully proximal-to-distal pitch. The hand often peaks before
  the forearm.

## The most common mistakes

These are read from the rows above, not separately sourced.

1. **A throw made by the arm alone.** The pelvis rotates earlier and further than the torso, and each segment starts
   as the one nearer the body peaks.
2. **Everything peaking together.** Pelvis, trunk and arm peaks are separated in time, by about 35 ms between pelvis
   and trunk by one derivation.
3. **A perfectly sequential textbook chain.** No pitch of 208 was fully proximal-to-distal, and the hand often peaks
   before the forearm.
4. **Hips and shoulders facing the same way at foot contact.** The gap is about 50-57° at foot contact in the pitchers
   measured, and it closes to about 12° by release.
5. **A slow cock-to-release.** The arm goes from about 175° external rotation to about 100° internal rotation in
   42-58 ms.
6. **A strike that stops short of full extension** (user-ruled 2026-10-25). A punch whose elbow is still bent at impact is
   not a punch; nor is a sword cut with the arm held in, or a kick that lands on a bent knee. The usual cause is a target
   placed where the limb cannot reach straight, so the solve folds the elbow to land on it: place the target at the
   extended limb's end instead, found with the bubble's max extension ("A strike at full extension" in the table below). A
   fold-0 angle point is not a straight elbow on a MetaHuman: its zero is the bind pose, about 37 degrees bent (2026-10-25).
7. **A punch with locked legs, or one that runs long.** More knee flexion at impact went with more punch force. A karate
   straight punch is done within 400 ms, a reverse punch in about 0.3 s. Do not take 45 ms as a punch's duration.

**Frame rate.** The important events are tens of milliseconds long. At `fps:60`, 0.145 s is about 9 frames and 42-58 ms
is about 2.5-3.5 frames (*derived*: frames = seconds x fps). At `fps:30` it is half that. The body-collision audit is
"not continuous collision detection", so check very fast motion at an appropriate fps
([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#body-collision-coverage)). A pitch has a very large external rotation (about 165-179°)
that a rig built for standing may not allow; widen the range in the rig as WALK-AND-RUN.md does for a gait, and read
the `WARNING joint limit` lines. The upper arm's turn about its own length is `axis=twist`. On the MetaHuman template,
**twist + turns the upper arm IN** (the forearm comes forward, measured 2026-10-13), and an arm cocked back over the
shoulder already reads at its outward stop (-73.8): the cock is the lay-back, and the strike is the turn in.

**Hips against shoulders.** WALK-AND-RUN.md's run example rig does not map pelvis turn or trunk twist; the MetaHuman
template ([Examples/MetaHumanBody.gcrig](Examples/MetaHumanBody.gcrig)) does: `pelvis` fold, side and twist (twist +
turns the body to its RIGHT, measured from native positions) and trunk twist on `spine_04`/`spine_05`. A right-handed
strike loads to the right (twist +) and drives to the left (twist -); turning the wrong way pulls the striking shoulder
back and the hand never comes across the body (the BlueEdge cut's first versions). Hip-shoulder separation is the pelvis turned
further than the trunk. `balance()` ([MOVE-BALANCE.md](MOVE-BALANCE.md)) keeps the mass over the planted feet; it is
static, so the push of a strike is still yours.

### A cut with a held blade

**THE SHORT FORM: the form taught since 2026-11-15.** It is the form of the slash the user accepted on 14 Nov:
the keys `Content/__MCPTests/SlashCheck/ThreePhase/TP_Keys.gcmove`, the cut `TP_W2.gcmove` in the same folder, and the
same two keys cut through a target in `TP_Two.gcmove`. Every line below is copied from those built files; every quoted
build line names the file it was copied from. The 22 Oct long form follows it, marked as the earlier form.

**Which form to start from.** A cut THROUGH A TARGET is two keys and the target line (step 4), and it is built
(`TP_Two`, 14 Nov). The slash the user accepted is the same two keys plus a third pose, without the target line
(`TP_W2`, step 6). Start from step 4.

A cut may also be ONE file (ruling 112): the keys written inline as poses in the cut's own `pose(steps)`, each solved alone as a
still with the cut's `collision()` and read into the list as a built key is (`PresetSlash_One.gcmove`; MOVE-PARAMETRIC-POSES.md,
"Two poses in one list"). The two-file form below is the same motion.

A cut is TWO files. A KEYS file, whose `build { }` makes each key as a one-frame clip (`A_TP_Cock = $Cock`, TP_Keys
6-10), and a CUT file, which reads the built keys and travels the blade between them. Both headers name the character,
`ik_rig:`, `controls:`, `physics_asset:`, `into:` and `fps:` (TP_Keys 1-5, TP_Two 1-5); ask
`gc_describe kind:"move" block:"header"` for what each carries.

1. **The target is a point**, in the body's words: `Bar = point(at=(forward=120, up=121))` (TP_Keys 15, TP_Two 11).
2. **The two keys**, each a one-frame clip on the same starting pose, both feet planted, the head on the target,
   collision on (TP_Keys 30-46):

   ```gcmove
   Floor = ground(z=0.85)
   Sword = surface(part=Sword)
   Face = frame(bone=head, toward=forward)
   Bar = point(at=(forward=120, up=121))
   # COCK: high and back on the sword side, the arm three quarters out
   CockHand = point(bone=upperarm_r, at=(right=57.2%, back=26.4%, up=39.8%), of=sword3_r, relative=character)
   # END OF SLASH: low-forward and across, past the bar
   SlashHand = point(bone=upperarm_r, at=(left=80.3%, forward=32.6%, down=124.5%), of=sword3_r, relative=character)
   FreeHandSlash = point(bone=upperarm_l, at=(left=14.3%, back=38.9%, down=97.7%), of=arm3_l, relative=character)
   BackWeight = point(at=(left=1.6, back=27.2))
   FrontWeight = point(at=(right=8.5, forward=4.8))
   Cock = clip(source=/Game/__MCPTests/SlashCheck/A_SlashIntent_Guard5, frames=1) {
     reach(sword3_r, to=$CockHand) [share=0.4]
     plant(stand_r, on=$Floor)
     plant(stand_l, on=$Floor)
     balance(pelvis, over=(stand_r, stand_l), max=35, at=$BackWeight)
     aim(neck, from=$Face, at=$Bar)
     collision(self, $Sword)
   }
   Slash = clip(source=/Game/__MCPTests/SlashCheck/A_SlashIntent_Guard5, frames=1) {
     reach(sword3_r, to=$SlashHand) [share=0.9]
     reach(arm3_l, to=$FreeHandSlash)
     plant(stand_r, on=$Floor)
     plant(stand_l, on=$Floor)
     balance(pelvis, over=(stand_r, stand_l), max=35, at=$FrontWeight)
     aim(neck, from=$Face, at=$Bar)
     collision(self, $Sword)
   }
   ```

   - **The wind-up is FOLDED.** Its hand is a share of the arm in a direction, from the shoulder at rest, about three
     quarters out (the file's own comment, line 18). The body joins a little (`[share=0.4]`), the weight goes toward the
     back foot (`at=$BackWeight`). It writes no blade line, no edge and no free arm of its own: the cut's travel lays the blade at every pose, this one included, so a level cut starts with the blade level and pointing back, and this pose looked at on its own shows the stance's blade, not the blade the cut will play. Check the place with the bubble on
     that key (`key:`, [GCMOVE.md](GCMOVE.md)), as for any place.
   - **The end of slash: its place is the bubble's max line toward its direction** ([GCMOVE.md](GCMOVE.md)). The trunk
     joins much more (`[share=0.9]`), the weight goes forward (`at=$FrontWeight`), the free hand is placed back by the
     hip.
   - *What the accepted file did, not the rule:* it wrote this hand past the arm's length (152% of the arm,
     `ThreePhase/ACCOUNT.md` line 10). A place past the chain laid straight is not met (THE CHECK BEFORE THE SOLVE,
     `gc_describe kind:"move" block:"constraints"`: "ends at least N cm short"), and it cost the weight: the key's build
     moved it "0.0 cm of the 12.8" (`ThreePhase/ACCOUNT.md` line 20).
   - **What the example copies**, each from the help's own text (ask the block named):
     - `Floor = ground(z=0.85)`: the floor's height in the character's space ("A `ground` is a HEIGHT and nothing
       else", `block:"places"`); 0.85 is this character's.
     - `balance(..., at=$BackWeight)`: "at= is a preferred horizontal projection in component space or relative to a
       supporting endpoint bone" of the centre of mass; `BackWeight` is a `point(at=)` in the character's space
       (`block:"balance"`, `block:"places"`).
     - `max=35`: "the additional anchor translation budget in centimetres, measured from this sample's ordinary solved
       pose" (`block:"balance"`).
     - `[share=]`: how much the trunk joins the reach, [The body joins a reach](HUMAN-GRASP-AND-REACH.md#the-body-joins-a-reach).
     - A share in a place (`57.2%`, `of=sword3_r`) is "that rig chain's length, its bones laid straight on the reference
       pose (the note gives the length)"; it resolves "on the body's reference pose, where its directions are the

4. **The cut** (TP_Two 9-32):

   ```gcmove

   Sword = surface(part=Sword)
   Bar = point(at=(forward=120, up=121))
   Cutting = point(bone=blade_r, at=(85, 0, 0))
   CockK = pose(source=/Game/__MCPTests/SlashCheck/ThreePhase/A_TP_Cock, from=reference) {

     plant(stand_l, on=$Floor)

     plant(stand_r, on=$Floor)
     plant(stand_l, on=$Floor)

   Cut = clip() {
   ```

5. **See the path first, then read the build's lines.** A cut is its blade's path, and two stills are only that path's

   ends ([GCMOVE.md](GCMOVE.md) step 6, "Judge the motion on its trails, never frame by frame"; how to read the

   and the stills never alone. Then the lines (TP_Two's in `ThreePhase/ACCOUNT.md` lines 34-38; the others as named):
   - **The arc line is the measure of a straight cut.** The accepted slash, `TP_W2`: "along one arc through its 3 poses,

5. **Speed:** a cut eases off the cock, is fastest about two thirds through, and stops hard at the landing. Time the
   travel by effort, `$Swing [accel=80%]` with `Swing = pose(steps) { $Start $End }` (POSES.md, "Time the travel by
   effort"; new in this build), or shape the end pose's amount curve by hand (POSES.md's `Strike` profile: shoulder
   peak 721 deg/s at 0.19 s of a 0.4 s cut, then a hard stop).

   **For a game, split the cut per action first** ([ANIMATION-LAYERS.md](ANIMATION-LAYERS.md) "Attacks and combos:
   one clip per action"): a rise, a downcut and the guard as separate clips joined by a montage's sections and
   blends avoid most of what follows. The rest of this step is for a cut that must stay one continuous take.

   **A hard landing on a cut of several poses (measured 2026-10-15, BlueEdge round 4, `BlueEdge_20261013/BUILD.md`
   "Motion round 4").** One effort-timed `pose(steps)` has ONE ramp for its whole travel. Where a joint turns back at a
   middle pose at speed (the cock between a turnover and a downcut), the law widens that ramp (to 4x, 0.2 s) for the whole
   travel, and the landing fades out over it: the blade is fastest a quarter into the downcut and eases into the floor,
   which reads as a placement, not a cut. The notes say it (`the ramp needed widening past 4x`); the solved-path capture
   says which joint. Two steps fixed it:
   - **Rest an instant where the cut turns back:** a `[pace=]` profile touching 0 at that pose
     (`Beats = profile(0=1, 0.63=1, 0.6667=0, 0.70=1, 1=1)` for the third of four poses) splits the travel into stages,
     each with its own clock and ramp (`in 2 stages, ... at rest between them`). Rest only where a real cut turns back:
     a rest at the top of the rise too made the turnover the cut's longest beat.
   - **Give the striking arm an athlete's ramp, with its source:** the template shoulder's `ramp=0.05` is the reference
     table's rule value (confidence low). A ballistic arm builds its push in 23-24 ms (simulated elbow, Rajagopal 2016,
     `Docs/Research/joint-speed-and-acceleration-references.md`), so the sword arm's `upperarm_r` takes `ramp=0.025` in
     the character's own `.gcrig`, speed and acceleration unchanged. The downcut then lands in ~0.25 s on a 0.07 s ramp
     and holds.
   - **The middle of a downcut needs no key.** A passing "strike" key bent the arm's path (jerk 404%) and slowed the cut;
     the direct path already passes the blade level at full extension. If a strike pose is wanted, put the hand on the
     nearly straight arm's line (ahead and a little below the shoulder) and let the wrist's 15 deg give the blade's last
     angle: a hand nearer than that asks the forearm to drop from a level upper arm and turns the shoulder past its stop.
   - **Coming back to the guard:** a straight path from a centre landing to a low-left guard sweeps the blade through the
     front leg, and round the outside of that leg is beyond the arm's reach. The cut's own path reversed (landing ->
     cock -> top -> guard) stays inside every limit; play it as a flourish at a lower effort.
   - **Body action at the top:** the free hand reaching out and back became the slowest joint on the downcut's clock
     (its ramp widened to 0.2 s again, the cut 0.94 s) and passed through the hip on the way back. Being tried instead
     (2026-10-15, result in BUILD.md when built): hip-shoulder separation, the hips turning square and sinking during
     the turnover while the chest stays coiled, on joints the downcut already moves.

**What did not work, in six builds, and why:**

- **Finding the end by tuning angles inside one effort-timed swing.** The hand kept finishing beside the wrong hip,
  because nobody had built or seen the end pose. Build it first.
- **Turning the upper arm in while the elbow is still bent** sweeps the forearm and the blade across the face. The
  turn in comes late, after the elbow has opened, as in a throw (above).
- **Wrist signs measured in another pose.** Fold + and side - tipped the blade forward at a low guard, but on the
  turned-in, palm-down arm at the landing they swung it back to hanging straight down. Let IK set the wrist from an aim.
- **A keyed angle as an offset.** `at(..., angle=)` sets the joint's reading and replaces that turn
  ([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#pass-through-a-pose-without-timing-it-transition-poses)).

## What is not known yet

- **Fleisig's pitching checklist values** (stride, lead knee, elbow, external rotation, abduction at foot contact, with
  youth and high-school figures) were seen only in search summaries. They are not given here. Candidates: Fleisig
  1996, *J Appl Biomech*, and an ISBS paper.
- **The boxing order** (leg drive, hip, shoulder, arm) with timings had no source that was read. Candidate: Lenetsky
  et al. 2020, "Defining the phases of boxing punches".
- **Other throws and kicks.** Only overarm baseball pitching is covered in detail. Putnam 1993 and the golf row say
  the outward order holds for other striking skills, with no numbers here for them.
- **Pelvis and trunk peak timing** is two different sets (25-39% / 51-52% against 12% / 36%), not reconciled.
- **Not in GraphCode:** a throw, a release event, a fall, dynamic balance (momentum); and, for a travel between
  key poses, a different EFFORT (`accel=`) per stretch. These remain planned. Built and published (2026-10-13/14):
  timing a travel by effort (`[accel=]` on the pose reference), hips-first lead and lag on that clock (`[phase=]`),
  passing an in-between pose at speed (a timed `pose(steps)` of three or more poses is joined through them, so a joint
  that keeps its direction does not stop), and the relative shape of the speed along the travel (`[pace=(0.25, 1)]`,
  a share of the move's own fastest point, not an effort; checked on the solved timing and the clip's keys, and the
  compressed clip plays the same pace). See POSES.md, "Time the travel by effort".
- **The struck side is built (2026-10-26):** a strike that lands on another person goes in with no stop and comes back,
  the struck head and neck give from the base of the neck and come back, and a lean the feet cannot hold takes one step,
  none of it written as a line (GCMOVE.md, the row for reaching ANOTHER PERSON; `PartnerTakesCross.gcmove`,
  `CrossLandsEffort.gcmove`). A fall is not.
- **Available in the 2026-10-13 native update:** `solid=collision` with several strictly separated enabled convex
  hulls, such as a sword's handle and blade. Each hull must be closed and convex; overlapping or touching hulls are
  refused by name because internal faces would give incorrect depth. The six native surface-solid tests pass.
- **Implemented, published and native-passed (2026-10-13):** pose readings via `[readings]` /
  `[readings=(0,last)]` on the build line (see ANIMATION.md); the engine fixture
  (`GraphCode.Move.Readings.SolvedWristAndToolPlaces`) passed on the 10:31Z shared publish. The sword's own cock and
  landing checks are deferred with the sword. See `Docs/GC-MOVE-SWING-PLAN.md` D1.
