# Human movement: turning while walking

[Human movement guides](HUMAN-MOVEMENT.md) · this page: how a person turns while walking.

This guide says how a person changes direction while walking: which foot strategy, how often, and in what order the
eyes, head, shoulders, pelvis and feet move. It gives timings and distances. Walking itself, with its foot rolls and
its body carried by the feet, is [WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md). That guide draws a straight walk. This guide says
what is and is not built for a turn, and does not repeat the walk mechanics.

**What the numbers are.** Almost every row is from an abstract read through Europe PMC. Baker et al. 2020 was read in full
text. *Cited* means the number was on the page that was read, and the row says abstract, results or table. *Derived*
means arithmetic on cited rows. Timings are relative to the event each row names. "Lead" means the head or eyes move
before the body. Copy a whole consistent set from one row. Never mix values from different studies into one pose.

## What a correct-looking result is

- **The eyes and head turn first.** They point at the new path before the body follows, and the body follows in
  order: head, shoulders, pelvis, feet.
- **The head starts the turn early,** about 1.1 m before the obstacle in one study, the same for turns of 45-135°.
- **A bigger turn is slower.** Walking speed falls as the turn angle grows.
- **The feet pivot toward the inside of the curve at heel strike,** and the trunk rolls toward the inside.
- **A step turn is wider and steadier than a spin turn.** Older people still mostly pick spin turns.
- **Turning is common.** In daily settings 8% to 50% of steps are turning steps, depending on the room.

### A walking turn: who moves first

### Turning on the spot

| Order or value | Population | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Order is eye, head, upper body, feet, with start times correlated | 5 adults. 45, 90 and 135° | cited (abstract), Hollands, Ziavra & Bronstein 2004 |
| Head, shoulder and pelvis start **together**, then the feet. The head turns faster and peaks earlier | Healthy older adults, 90° | cited (abstract), Akram, Frank & Fraser 2010 (on the spot) |

### The two foot strategies

Definitions (Hase & Stein 1999, abstract). Take the case where the right foot is in front and braking.

- **Spin turn:** turn right by spinning on the right foot, toward the side of the stance foot.
- **Step turn:** turn left. Weight goes onto the right leg, the left hip rotates outward, the left foot steps out in
  the new direction, and turning continues until the right leg steps that way.

The step turn is more stable because its base of support is wider (Hase & Stein 1999).

| Value | Population and convention | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Some subjects used the step turn in every part of the gait cycle. Foot placement and trunk rotation changed **within one step after the cue**. The walking rhythm usually did **not reset** | Healthy adults, turns cued by nerve stimulation | cited (abstract), Hase & Stein 1999 |
| Two kinds of spin turn: "ipsilateral crossover" and "ipsilateral pivot". Both need more rotation range and more effort than a step turn, which keeps a wide base | Small sample, 90° turns | cited (abstract), Taylor, Dabnichki & Strike 2005 |
| Older adults **prefer spin turns**. They prefer step turns only for **90° turns while walking fast** | Healthy older adults. 45° and 90° turns. 3 speeds | cited (abstract), Akram, Frank & Chenouri 2010 |
| Turning steps are **8-50%** of all steps, depending on the course (parking lot 8%, store 35%, office 45%, cafeteria 50%) | 11 able-bodied adults, filmed in daily settings. % of steps | cited (abstract), Glaister et al. 2007 |

### A sharp or a wide corner

| Value | Population | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp turn: radius **50 cm in 2 steps**. Wide turn: radius **200 cm in 5-7 steps**. About 0.4 g sideways, tilting the felt gravity direction about 21°. **Head rolls up to 8°** ahead of it. Head yaw leads the path by **up to 25°**. **The trunk does not anticipate**. Eyes roll 1-1.5° | 5 subjects | cited (abstract), Imai et al. 2001 |

## Angles and distances

The sources give timings and orders far more than angles. The angles and distances that exist are:

| Value | Where |
|---|---|
| Head starts the turn about **1.1 m** before the obstacle | Sreenivasa 2008 (above) |
| Head yaw leads the path by up to **25°** in a sharp corner. Head roll up to **8°** | Imai 2001 (above) |
| Head leads body yaw by about **200 ms** | Courtine & Schieppati 2003 (above) |
| Head against pelvis at peak head speed: about **54°/s** (visible target), **25°/s** (remembered) | Solomon 2006 (above) |
| Corner radius **50 cm** (2 steps) or **200 cm** (5-7 steps) | Imai 2001 (above) |
| Head yaw range, neck rotation, trunk twist and head speed in a gaze shift | [HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md](HUMAN-GAZE-AND-HEAD.md) and the joint-range guide linked from [HUMAN-MOVEMENT.md](HUMAN-MOVEMENT.md) |

**Where the sources disagree.** All of these are shown, not reconciled:

- **How far the head leads.** About 1 s (Grasso), about 200 ms (Courtine), and eyes 407 ms with head 50 ms (Baker).
  These are measured from different events (corner, body yaw, foot contact), so they do not truly conflict. Sreenivasa
  found the lead is a fixed distance (about 1.1 m), not a fixed time.
- **Turning on the spot.** Young adults (Hollands 2004, n = 5) turn in strict order eye, head, trunk, feet. Older
  adults (Akram 2010) start head, shoulders and pelvis together.
- **Step against spin turns.** The step turn is the more stable strategy (Hase & Stein, Taylor), yet older adults mostly
  chose spin turns (Akram 2010).
- **What comes first.** Patla, Adkin & Ballard 1999 put a centre-of-mass move (foot placement or trunk roll) first,
  then the head. Other rows put the eyes or head first. They are different tasks, cues and populations, and the note
  does not reconcile them. This guide's author noticed the pairing; it is not the note's own comparison.

## The most common mistakes

All are read from the rows above.

1. **The body turns as one block, with the head and eyes not leading.** In every study the eyes or head start the turn
   first, or at the same time at the earliest.
2. **The same turn speed for every angle.** Walking speed falls steadily as the turn angle grows (Sreenivasa).
3. **A turn with no foot strategy.** The sources describe a step turn (a wide step out into the new direction) and a
   spin turn (spinning on the stance foot). Neither is a body that simply slides around.
4. **The trunk leaning into a sharp corner before the head does.** The trunk does not anticipate; the head leads
   (Imai).
5. **Turning that restarts the walking rhythm.** The walking rhythm usually did not reset when a turn was cued (Hase &
   Stein).
6. **A turn that waits several steps after the cue.** Foot placement and trunk rotation changed within one step after
   the cue (Hase & Stein).

## How to author it in GraphCode

[WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md) owns the walk. It draws a straight gait: legs in place, each foot planted with
`roll=`, the body carried by `drive=Root`. **It has no turn.** Nothing in the guides builds a curved walk, a spin turn
or a step turn. What follows is what exists, and where the gaps are.

| You want | What exists | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A straight walk to turn from | [WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md) | Keep the walk's feet rolling and the body carried by them |
| The head and eyes leading | `swing` on neck/head bones or `aim()` on a head chain; native MetaHuman Face curves for the eyes | [METAHUMAN-RIG.md](METAHUMAN-RIG.md) names the eye-look curves and their remaining calibration limits. A shared head/eye look-target solver is not supplied. Order the authored phases: eyes, head, shoulders, pelvis, feet |
| The trunk and shoulders following | `swing(spine_0N, axis=twist, by=...)` with a later `profile` | WALK-AND-RUN.md says a walk needs ±13° of trunk counter-turn and a run ±21°, and writes `spine_0N [..., twists=(-15, 15)]` in the rig ([step 1](WALK-AND-RUN.md#1-give-the-rig-foot-contacts-that-ride-their-own-bones)). A turn asks for a different range that no guide gives. Widen the range in the rig, and read the `WARNING joint limit` lines |
| A step turn | `swing(thigh_X, axis=twist, ...)` and `axis=side` for the stepping leg, and a `plant` for the stance leg | Hip rotation needs `twists=` in the rig, and the sign must be checked with a one-pose test (WALK-AND-RUN.md). Not built as a turn |
| A spin turn on a planted foot | A `plant(leg, on=$Floor, roll=(...), drive=Root, weight=...)` held through the turn | WALK-AND-RUN.md says the planted foot's heading follows the drawing, and it turns about its held point (under 0.5 cm at the toes for a degree-sized turn). A 90° spin on a held foot has not been built or measured |
| The path itself, the body's heading | `swing(root, axis=twist, by=...)` on a rig with that axis, or native game movement/Sequencer | [METAHUMAN-RIG.md](METAHUMAN-RIG.md) documents root/pelvis twist. Choose one travel owner and inspect [root extraction/locking](ANIMATION.md#root-travel-and-visible-body-height-in-a-game); heading alone does not re-plan footfalls into a turn |
| A character that turns in a game | Blend Spaces with direction, AnimBP states for starts, stops and turns, Aim Offset for looking | [ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#build-the-runtime-performance-with-native-animation-tools). These are native Unreal assets, not `.gcmove` lines |

**Pelvis and trunk yaw: on the MetaHuman template, not the run example.** WALK-AND-RUN.md's full-body run example rig
does not map pelvis tilt, list and turn, or trunk twist and side bend yet. The shipped MetaHuman template
([Examples/MetaHumanBody.gcrig](Examples/MetaHumanBody.gcrig)) declares `pelvis` fold, side and twist (signs measured from native positions:
fold + tips the body forward, side + leans it to its left, twist + turns it to its RIGHT: +15 brings the left hand
7.9 cm forward; a picture read once said left, backwards) and trunk twist on
`spine_04`/`spine_05` (12.5 degrees each, 3 on the lower three). A turn needs them.

**Balance is static.** `balance()` keeps the mass over the planted soles ([MOVE-BALANCE.md](MOVE-BALANCE.md)); the lean
into a curve (about 0.4 g in a sharp corner) is dynamic and is authored by hand and judged by eye.

**Judge a turn by playing it at its real speed** and from more than one side, with the feet over a floor
([WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md#5-build-read-the-note-and-look)).

## What is not known yet

- **The proportion of step against spin turns in young adults** is not quantified in any abstract that was read.
- **Head yaw onset 800 ± 200 ms before the corner pivot** appeared only as a search snippet. It is not used.
- **"Eyes led (407 ms prior to IFC1), followed by the head (50 ms prior to IFC1)"** also appears as a listing quote for
  Kim 2021 whose abstract has no numbers and does not define "IFC1". The note judged the figures to agree with Baker
  2020, whose results are used above.
- **Trunk and pelvis onset means** in Baker 2020 are in a figure only and were not read as numbers.
- **No automatic whole-body turning recipe:** root/pelvis twist, head aim, native face curves and
  [balance](MOVE-BALANCE.md) are available primitives. A coordinated curved gait, planted-foot spin
  and a shared calibrated head/eye target still require authored composition and verification.
  The earlier claim that root heading, pelvis yaw, eye curves and balance were all absent was stale.
