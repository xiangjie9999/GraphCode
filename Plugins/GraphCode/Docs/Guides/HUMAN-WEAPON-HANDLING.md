# Human movement: holding and handling a rifle or a pistol

This guide says what a person's body does with a gun, in angles, distances and order of events. It does not say how
to build the grip in GraphCode. That is [HANDS.md](HANDS.md), and this guide points into it.

**What the numbers are.** They come from US Army field manuals and from named shooting instructors. A manual
teaches what soldiers should do. It is *doctrine*, not a measurement of people. No source here measured the joint
angles of a person holding a weapon. Every row says where it came from. Where two sources differ, both are shown.
Angles are in degrees.

## What a correct-looking result is

- The rifle is held by the pistol grip and the handguard, not the barrel. The butt stock sits in the shoulder pocket.
- The head stays upright and the rifle comes up to it. The head does not drop down to the rifle.
- The trunk leans forward toward the target. Nobody stands vertical behind a gun.
- At rest the trigger finger lies straight along the frame, outside the trigger guard. It goes to the trigger last.
- A pistol is held with both hands high on the grip, the support hand filling the space the firing hand leaves.
- Reloads happen up in front of the chin, with the eyes on the target.

## The order of events

None of the sources gives a duration for any of these. The orders are doctrine. Do not invent timings for them: take
the timing from the action you are animating.

### A rifle, from slung to ready

Positions from TC 3-22.9 paragraph 6-13 onward (pp. 95-101), in the order a shooter moves through them.

| Position | Rifle | Hands | When it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hang | Slung, safety on | Free | Hands needed for other tasks, no threat |
| Safe hang | Slung, safety on | Firing hand on the pistol grip, finger off the trigger | No immediate threat |
| Collapsed low ready | Slung, held close for muzzle control (the manual's figure shows the exact hold; it was not read) | Firing hand on the grip, non-firing hand on the handguard or foregrip | Crowds, weapon retention |
| Low ready | Slung, butt stock in the shoulder, **muzzle 30-45° down** toward the sector; head upright | Index finger straight, out of the trigger guard | Ready to engage |
| High ready | Slung, **butt stock in the armpit**, **muzzle at least 45° up** | Firing hand as in low ready | Threats overhead, elevated sector |
| Ready (up) | Toe of the butt stock in the shoulder, muzzle toward the threat, stock weld established | Non-firing hand on the handguard | Contact imminent |

### A pistol draw, five steps

TC 3-23.35 pp. 59-64.

1. Grip high and firm with the firing hand. The support hand anchors to the body.
2. Defeat the holster's retention and pull straight up.
3. As the muzzle clears the holster, **drop the firing elbow and rotate the muzzle to the target**.
4. The hands meet. The support fingers slide "under and against the trigger guard". The heel of the support hand goes
   against the grip. The firing thumb goes on top of the support thumb. Then the support wrist locks (pp. 63-64).
5. Extend the arms toward the target.

### A pistol reload

TC 3-23.35 p. 127 and Rackley, Athlon Outdoors 2019.

1. Work in the workspace in front of the chin (see "Distances" below). The eyes stay on the target.
2. Draw the new magazine with the index finger along its front ("finger toward bullets").
3. Hold the pistol and the magazine at the same angle and index the magazine into the well.
4. The gun is at eye level. The strong thumb hits the magazine release as the other hand reaches.
5. The palm seats the magazine. The slide is released or racked.

## Joint angles, distances and positions

### Rifle, the body

Source for every row: TC 3-22.9 (US Army, 2016, Change 3). Page numbers are PDF pages.

| Element | What the body does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Firing hand | Pistol grip in the "V" between thumb and index finger. Back strap "high in the web" of the hand. The other three fingers close with no gap. Pressure "similar to a firm handshake", "firm but relaxed" | p. 91 |
| Trigger finger, firing | No fixed joint. The finger "will lay naturally across the trigger after achieving proper grip" (fig. 8-2). "Place their finger on the trigger where it naturally falls" | pp. 113-114, 139-140 |
| Trigger finger, not firing | "indexed on the lower receiver, well outside the trigger guard". After firing, "keep it straight along the lower receiver placed outside of the trigger guard". Rule 3: "Keep Finger Straight and Off the Trigger Until Ready to Fire" | pp. 22, 91, 147 |
| Support (non-firing) hand | Holds the handguard (not the barrel) as far forward as naturally possible "to aid in recoil management". If possible the thumb gives downward force on the handguard | p. 91, p. 106 (the two snippets word the forward limit slightly differently) |
| Support hand, practitioner | Palm under the fore-end with the elbow down for standing and kneeling. Hand extended forward on free-floated rails. Thumb along the side pointing at the target, or over the top with the index finger pointing at the target | Flasowski, Shooting Illustrated 2012 |
| Firing elbow, standing | "Tucked toward the shooter's side" | pp. 105-106 |
| Butt stock | In the firing shoulder pocket. Standing: "highest point of shoulder". Kneeling or sitting: "mid-point" to "mid to high". High enough "to allow for an upright head position". Its height "will vary from firing position to firing position" | pp. 92, 105-112 |
| Stock (cheek) weld | "the full weight of the head on the stock". Head "as upright as possible". "bring the rifle up to your head, not your head down to the rifle" | p. 92 |
| Standing stance | Feet shoulder-width apart. "Aggressive lean forward toward the target" | pp. 105-106 |
| Natural point of aim | "where the barrel naturally orients when the shooter's muscles are relaxed and support is achieved". Checked by closing the eyes through one breath and reopening | pp. 93-94 |
| Recoil management | From a stable position: "the weight of their body, and the placement of the weapon". After the shot the head stays on the stock, the firing eye stays open, the trigger is held back through recoil | para. 6-8, 6-9 (p. 94), p. 144 |

### Rifle, kneeling, sitting and prone

TC 3-22.9.

| Position | What the body does | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| Kneeling | Non-firing leg bent "approximately 90 degrees at the knee", directly under the rifle. Non-firing elbow in front of or behind the kneecap, triceps on the knee, "Elbow NOT ON KNEE". Slight lean toward the target. Stretch kneeling: all weight on the non-firing foot, firing leg stretched behind | 108-109 |
| Sitting | Face the target "at a 10- to 30-degree angle". Feet about shoulder width. Firing elbow inside the firing knee. Non-firing elbow on the leg below or at the bend of the knee | 110-112 |
| Prone | Legs spread, heels flat, or the firing-side leg bent at the knee "to relieve pressure on the stomach". Non-firing elbow on the ground | 90, 113-116, 223 |

### Pistol, the body

Sources: TC 3-23.35 (US Army), FM 3-23.35 (2003), and the practitioner pieces named in the last column.

| Element | What the body does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Firing hand | Grip in the "V" between thumb and index, firm handshake pressure. "high on the pistol grip", web "against the beavertail notch", "as close as possible to the line of bore" | TC 3-23.35 pp. 68, 72-73 |
| Two-hand grip | "Support hand provides most grip pressure". Support hand "high on the pistol grip, without contacting the slide". Its pressure "will run 45 degrees to the firing hand's grip". Support thumb parallel to the frame, "extended as far forward as possible" | TC 3-23.35 pp. 70, 72, 74, 77, 79-80 |
| Thumbs forward | Shooting thumb off the exposed grip panel so the heel of the support hand fills it. Both thumbs forward. Hand as high in the tang as possible. 360° contact | Spaulding, Guns & Ammo 2023 |
| Thumbs, alternatives | Thumbs forward (side by side or one over the other) or "thumb over" (support thumb locks the shooting thumb down). Either is used successfully | Christian, SSUSA 2018 |
| Trigger finger, firing | Army wording: contact "between the tip and second joint". After the shot the finger moves forward but keeps contact with the trigger. Practitioner wording puts it on the pad of the last finger segment. The Marine Corps manual (MCRP 3-01B) asks only for a placement that lets the finger move independently straight to the rear, and says it varies by person | TC 3-23.35 pp. 68, 92, 95; FM 3-23.35 para. 2-1a, 2-4b |
| Trigger finger, not firing | "Keep your trigger finger straight and pressed along the frame". Outside the guard when reholstering | TC 3-23.35 pp. 65, 128; NRA rule 2, Quandahl 2024 |
| Arms | Wrists locked. Arms extended toward the target "without locking out the elbows" (TC 3-23.35). FM 3-23.35 (2003) taught the firing wrist and elbow locked. This is a change of doctrine, not two values to average | TC 3-23.35 pp. 77, 79-80; FM 3-23.35 para. 2-6b |
| Standing stance | Feet about shoulder width, support foot slightly ahead, knees slightly bent, weight on the balls of the feet. "shoulders slightly forward of the buttocks", upper torso and shoulders "forward of your buckle". "Aggressive lean forward toward the target" | TC 3-23.35 pp. 60, 67, 77, 86, 128 |
| Head and arms | Head level, not tilted. Target seen "directly in line with the arm and sights". Arms centred on the chest or slightly offset to the dominant-eye side | TC 3-23.35 p. 85 |
| Eye focus | Centre the front sight in the rear sight and focus on the front sight | FM 3-23.35 para. 2-2a |
| Ready | Two-hand grip, upper arms close to the body, **forearms about 45°** (muzzle toward the target's centre while moving) | TC 3-23.35 p. 75; FM 3-23.35 para. 2-6a |
| Crouch | Boxer's stance, knees bent slightly, trunk bent forward from the hips | FM 3-23.35 para. 2-6d |

### The four pistol stances

Christian, Shooting Illustrated 2021, and Wikipedia ("Weaver stance") for the weight split.

| Stance | Feet | Arms | Trunk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isosceles | Square to the target, shoulder width (SSUSA: slightly wider) | Both fully extended, gun in front of the eyes | Shoulders a bit forward, head upright |
| Power isosceles | Gun-side foot back 8-10 in (20-25 cm; SSUSA 10-12 in), knees unlocked | Both fully extended | Forward lean |
| Weaver | Support foot 8-10 in (20-25 cm) forward, toes at the target. Strong toes turned 45° out. Most weight on the front foot, front knee slightly bent, rear leg nearly straight | Strong arm not fully extended, elbow unlocked and slightly out. **Support elbow bent, pointing down**. Push forward with the strong hand, pull back with the support hand | Leaning forward at the hips, support shoulder into the gun |
| Chapman (modified Weaver) | As Weaver, support foot less far forward | Strong arm fully extended, support arm bent | Many drop the cheek toward the biceps |

| Value | Source |
|---|---|

That is a 30-46 cm sphere whose centre is about 30 cm in front of the chin, so the eyes stay on the threat | TC 3-22.9 p. 141; the same definition is TC 3-23.35 para. 8-5 |

## The most common mistakes

These are what looks wrong. All six are *derived* from the rows above, not separately sourced.

1. **The head ducked down to the rifle.** The manual says the opposite: bring the rifle up to the head, with the head
   as upright as possible.
2. **The trigger finger inside the guard at a ready position.** At ready it is straight, outside the guard, along the
   receiver or the frame.
3. **The butt stock below the shoulder pocket at "ready (up)".** The toe of the stock is in the shoulder.
4. **A vertical, upright trunk with no forward lean.** Both manuals ask for an "aggressive lean forward toward the
   target".
5. **A pistol held with straight locked elbows in a Weaver.** In a Weaver the strong arm is not fully extended, and
   the support elbow is bent and points down. (In an isosceles both arms are extended.)
6. **A reload done at the belt with the eyes dropped to the gun.** The reload is done in the chin-centred workspace
   with the eyes on the target.

### Low ready: two numbers, both shown

HANDS.md Step 9 places the rifle with `reach(arm_R, from=$ButtFrame, to=$LowReady)` and
`reach(arm_L, contact=Palm_L, to=$GuardFrame)`, then `collision(self, $Gun)`. Its `LowReady` frame points the barrel
"27 degrees down and 38 across, a patrol carry". The across angle was chosen so the support hand could reach the
handguard (pointed 15 degrees across, the left hand ended 18 cm short).

TC 3-22.9 gives a low ready of **30-45° down** and gives no across angle. So the shipped example is about 3° shallower
than the lower edge of doctrine. Neither number is wrong. They answer different questions. If you need a doctrine
low ready, measure a new `toward=` on your rifle and rig and read the reach note; do not copy the example's numbers.

### Stance and trunk

- **Forward lean.** Swing the spine bones on `axis=fold`. On a rig built like RifleMan a positive fold leans the trunk
  forward ([WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md) maps trunk flexion to `spine_01..03` fold). Check the sign with a
  one-pose render first.
- **Feet.** Leg swings (`swing(thigh_r, axis=fold, ...)`, `swing(calf_r, axis=fold, ...)`) set knees "slightly bent".
  `plant(leg, on=$Floor, contact=Sole, ...)` holds a foot where it stands ([POSES.md](POSES.md#floors-and-travel)).
  Without `drive=` a plant holds a foot under body movement you author yourself.
- **Check the foot spacing in a render.** An orthographic side view with `ortho_width` set makes pixels centimetres
  ([WALK-AND-RUN.md](WALK-AND-RUN.md#5-build-read-the-note-and-look)). Use it to read a 20-25 cm foot offset.
- **Hold the pose, do not freeze it.** A held weapon should breathe and settle a little, not drift
  ([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#natural-movement-is-part-of-the-performance)).

### Draw and reload

[HANDS.md](HANDS.md) has no draw or reload recipe, and nobody has built one. The five-step draw and the reload order
above are targets to check a result against. They can be authored with the verbs the guides already show (`reach`,
`pose(steps)`, `collision`), but that has not been tried. Do not claim it works until it has been built and looked at
from three sides.

## What is not known yet

- **The manuals were read as search snippets.** The archive's OCR search returns sentences, not pages. So figure 8-2
  and the exact hold of the collapsed low ready were not read.
- **Doctrine is not measurement.** The angles are what soldiers are taught: kneeling knee about 90°, pistol forearms
  about 45°, rifle muzzle 30-45° down at low ready. No measured joint angles of a person holding a weapon are in the
  source.
- **No durations.** None of the draw, reload or ready transitions has a source for how long it takes.
- **No eye control in GraphCode.** The guides show no verb that places the eyes. "Focus on the front sight" cannot be
  authored as an eye movement. Head position is a neck swing or an `aim` on the head chain
  ([ANIMATION.md](ANIMATION.md#what-each-implemented-instruction-does)). Check the result in a render.
- **Balance is static.** `balance()` keeps the mass over the planted soles ([MOVE-BALANCE.md](MOVE-BALANCE.md)), and
  `at=` says where over them the weight should sit (the balls of the feet); a held weapon carries no mass in it yet.
  A forward lean is authored by hand and judged by eye.
- **No rifle trigger-finger recipe.** HANDS.md Step 7 closes all four fingers on the rifle. The finger-straight-along-
  the-receiver rest has no recipe in the guides. The pistol equivalent is `IndexAlong` in Step 6.
- **Trigger-finger placement varies.** The Army, the practitioners and the Marine Corps word it three ways. See the
  ladder in [HANDS.md](HANDS.md#holding-any-weapon-the-order-and-what-to-do-when-the-hand-cannot-reach) for what to do
  when the hand cannot reach.
