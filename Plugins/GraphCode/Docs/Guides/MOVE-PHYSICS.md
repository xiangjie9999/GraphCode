# Move gravity, collision and body contact

Native implementation introduced 2026-09-29. No character Blueprint or Animation Blueprint is generated by these features.

## Animation floor and live world

| Authoring | Output | Environment |
|---|---|---|
| `clip(gravity=true, collision=$Floor)` | Corrected native AnimSequence tracks | Explicit `Floor = ground(z=0)` in animation/component space |
| `constraints(gravity=true, collision=world)` | Native Control Rig | Actual world collision at playback time |

`gravity=false` still requests physics evaluation, with zero gravitational acceleration. Omitting
`gravity`, `physics_profile`, `physics()` and physical floor `contact()` preserves the non-physics animation path.
Body-surface `contact(chain,contact=Pad,on=self)` uses IK and does not enable physical simulation. `from=$Clip`
inherits these settings; an explicit value on the derived clip overrides the inherited setting.
Gravity belongs to the clip environment. A pose contribution of 40% does not mean 40% gravity.
`gravity=true` enables the native Rig Physics solver's standard gravity. Custom world gravity
overrides are not currently read automatically by the generated rig.

The Physics Asset comes from the existing `character: ... part: ...` binding, or the selected
preview mesh. Optional `physics_asset:/Game/.../PA_Name` selects another native model for this
Move document without changing the mesh/component. Its bodies, exclusions, mass, joint frames, limits and drives supply the physical
model. An explicitly kinematic body stays kinematic. Default and simulated bodies simulate.
Disconnected simulated islands and missing/unsupported geometry are refused.
The asset's native `SolverSettings` also supply position/velocity/projection iterations, collision
cull distance, depenetration velocity, fixed timestep, joint solver choice and contact manifolds.
Adjust those asset settings when a physical model needs more accuracy; they are not new Move verbs.

The installed UE 5.7 Control Rig Physics engine plugin supports boxes, capsules and spheres.
Convex bodies are refused, not silently omitted or approximated. Its live world interaction is
one-way and its installed implementation does not support ISM world collision. This is an
experimental native engine feature. A successful bounded impact test is not arbitrary-speed CCD.

Floor baking uses solver-owned collision available from the first step and a fresh simulation
per build. The bake runs at at least 60 samples per second while preserving duration, scalar curves
and authored arrival timing. Final joint-limit and swept collision checks run before any asset is
saved. Infeasible output is refused; increasing drive strength does not bypass those checks.
Attached `surface()` geometry is not yet supported by physics baking.

During a bake, the native Physics Asset joints constrain simulation. Move corrects physical
compliance against the anatomical limits declared by the IK Rig and reports the largest correction
and sample per joint. The corrected tracks must pass the independent joint and collision audits;
a correction that creates penetration cannot be saved as a successful animation.
Remaining physical self-contact is fitted from the preceding accepted pose using the same swept
form solver. A blocked joint stops at contact while other motion can continue; the maximum change
and affected sample count are reported. Joint limits are checked again after this correction.
For an explicit animation floor, Move resolves remaining penetration by raising the complete pose
by the measured depth. This includes the swept motion between keys, preserves joint angles and
relative body positions, and reports the largest placement correction. Contact diagnostics measure
the final corrected tracks; another body blocking a requested contact remains visible as a warning.

## Preserving the intended pose

Gravity alone produces passive motion. Native joint drives can preserve relative articulation,
but a freely rotating torso also needs an orientation target for a controlled roll.

The character's native joint drives and per-body Physical Animation defaults supply normal
following. Configure those once in its Physics Asset. A default Physical Animation entry has
`ProfileName=None` (the canonical projection omits that default name). Keep `None` in the asset's
`PhysicalAnimationProfiles` list so native profile maintenance retains it. Move does not use PHAT's
editor-only current selection or choose the first named preset.

`physics_profile=Name` optionally overrides these defaults with an existing **Physical Animation
profile on the Physics Asset**. Bodies absent from the override retain their default settings;
a name with no entries is refused:

```gcmove
Turn = clip(frames=90, gravity=true, collision=$Floor, physics_profile=FollowForm) {
  $AuthoredForm
}
```

This asset profile is distinct from Move's scalar `profile(t=value, ...)`. Its orientation and
position stiffness, velocity damping and force caps map to stock Physics Controls. A local profile
follows the nearest physical ancestor. A non-local profile follows the component-space animated
target. There are no built-in human strength values.

An included `pose(collision=self)` still fits its requested articulation to self contact before
physics begins. Floor support is deferred to physics so gravity can move the whole character while
the form turns. The final collision check runs again after simulation and joint-limit correction.

Collision fitting projects each trial articulation onto the selected IK rig's joint limits before
checking its swept path. A collision-stopped blend must still respect locked axes. Final track
interpolation is audited separately; an unsatisfied limit refuses the animation instead of saving
an invalid pose.

For freely falling but pose-guided movement, use orientation drives and leave global position
strength at zero. For an explicit contact constraint, the selected body needs positive
`PositionStrength` in its default Physical Animation entry or the optional override. Contact
defines the surface normal itself; the ordinary following settings may be local or non-local.
Missing settings name the affected body. With no drives, Move warns that the result is passive
physics. A named profile is not required by `contact()`.

## Choosing a supporting body

```gcmove
Floor = ground(z=0)
Acquire = profile(0=0, 0.2=1, 0.75=1, 1=0)

SupportedForm = pose(collision=$Floor) {
  # Other pose instructions determine orientation and articulation.
  contact(pelvis, on=$Floor, weight=$Acquire)
}

Motion = clip(frames=90, gravity=true) {
  $SupportedForm
}
```

`pelvis` here is an example **body bone in the character's Physics Asset**. It is not a reserved
body-part keyword or an IK chain. `contact()` acquires surface-normal support through a native
Physics Control. It does not pin one material point to the floor: the supporting location is
recomputed from the selected body's collision geometry as its orientation changes. This allows
side-to-back transitions without switching between hardcoded activity verbs.
The character must have that body's position drive configured as described above.

The pose contribution and `weight=` multiply. Identical body/floor goals combine by their strongest
active contribution; incompatible floor heights are refused. Weight zero releases the normal goal
back to its ordinary animation target. Collision remains active throughout. Tangential movement and
orientation continue to follow the authored motion with their configured drive strengths.

Physics controls have compliance. Give acquisition time through the weight curve. If a full-weight
goal remains above its named floor, the build reports the body, target, worst separation and sample.
Other body geometry may block the requested contact; revise the form or physical model rather than
forcing penetration. The final collision audit is independent of this contact diagnostic.

Explicit physical floor `contact()` belongs to animation baking. Runtime `constraints()` supports
gravity and passive world support; it does not yet compile selected-body contact acquisition against
arbitrary world surfaces. A baked floor height must never substitute for that missing runtime query.

## Letting selected parts yield

```gcmove
Legs = bones(thigh_l, calf_l, foot_l, ball_l, thigh_r, calf_r, foot_r, ball_r)
Release = profile(0=0, 0.25=0, 0.4=1, 0.65=1, 0.85=0, 1=0)

LooseLegs = pose() {
  physics($Legs, weight=$Release)
}

Motion = clip(frames=150, gravity=true, collision=$Floor) {
  $AuthoredForm
  $LooseLegs
  contact(pelvis, on=$Floor, weight=$Acquire)
}
```

The example names existing body bones explicitly. `physics(body, weight=...)` also selects one
body. Omitted weight means 1; constants may use `100%`, while profile stops use unitless 0..1.
Zero preserves the configured animation-following drives. One releases animation-target spring
and damping forces, including native joint drives. Intermediate amounts reduce those forces in
the solver. They do not blend an unchecked pose after collision or change gravity's acceleration.
The physical state is retained when a curve releases or restores following.

Native joints, angular limits, body damping, self collision and the declared environment collision
remain active. Unselected bodies keep their configured drives. Explicitly kinematic or missing
bodies are reported; selection does not silently rewrite their Physics Asset. A body can remain
supported by another body or a joint without reaching the floor.

Collision prevents overlap; it does not attract a hand to the torso or choose which surface it
should rest on. Author the starting form above the intended support, then let the relevant joints
yield. Releasing only the wrist leaves a driven elbow free to hold the hand above the body.
Conversely, weak following on an unselected shoulder can let the whole arm sag beside the torso.
Check the native body settings and both the visible skin and collision shapes. A fingertip touching
first can stop the fall before the palm lies flat; `physics()` alone does not impose a palm-rest goal.
For deliberate placement, the body-surface variant below requests a pad position, with optional facing.
Do not describe a passive hand/torso test as proof of that targeted feature.

Pose contributions multiply the physics weight. Overlapping physics instructions take their
strongest contribution per body. A child clip replaces inherited physics instructions on the same
body while preserving its contact and IK goals. Nested `at(frame=..., speed=.../arrive=...)` points
use the existing movement timing rules. Contact remains an independent surface goal: releasing
animation following does not cancel explicitly requested contact.

The saved animation carries `GC_PhysicsWeight_<bone>` float curves. Generated native physics rigs
have matching curve inputs, initially zero, and apply participation before each solver step. Use
the existing animation integration to transfer those curves; explicitly supply zero when the next
clip has no release curve, so a previous clip's value is not retained. No character or Animation
Blueprint is generated. This curve input supports runtime partial physics; selected-body contact
acquisition against arbitrary runtime world surfaces remains the separate limitation above.

## A relaxed part: `dynamic=` on the rig, `physics()` in the clip

How freely a part moves is a fact about the body, so it is written once in the rig, on the joint:
`dynamic=` from 0 (only animation moves it) to 1 (it swings free). The rig's `physics_asset()` output
writes it into the asset as a joint drive toward the clip's pose and a body damping. The clip says
which parts to hand to physics now, with `physics()`, and `gravity=true`.

```gcrig
joints {
  hip
  knee  [turns_in=$knee_back, range=(-5, 145), closes=back, dynamic=1]
}
build {
  PHAT_DenseRig = physics_asset()
}
```

```gcmove
Drop = clip(frames=90, gravity=true) {
  $Folded
  physics(knee, weight=0)
}
```

`$Folded` stands for whatever poses the knee into its fold: a leg hanging straight gives gravity no
lever, so an unposed knee would not move at any amount.

**On a physics asset its rig built with `dynamic=` on any joint, only the bodies a `physics()` line
or a physical `contact()` names simulate; every other body is kinematic and follows the animation
exactly.** Above, the knee swings and the hip follows the animation. An asset made by hand,
and a rig-built one whose rig declares nothing, keep the rule of the section above: every body that
is not explicitly kinematic simulates.

What the build says, per declared joint:

- one that simulated: what it did, measured from the bake, e.g. `knee dynamic=1: moved at most
  65.09 degrees from the clip's pose in this clip`. No frequency is printed: the
  engine's inertia conditioning makes a number worked out from the drive differ from the swing.
- one no `physics()` names: ``wrist_r declares `dynamic=0.75` but no physics() names it: it follows
  the animation``.
- a weight reaching 100% on a joint declared between 0 and 1: `weight 100% releases wrist_r's
  declared drive completely; use a smaller weight to keep it`. At 100% the declared amount no longer
  matters; body damping, limits and collision still act.

And it refuses:

- `physics()` on a joint declared `dynamic=0`: ``wrist_r declares `dynamic=0`: only animation moves it``.
- `physics()` on a joint the skeleton's rig declared when the clip's `physics_asset` does not carry
  the declaration. An asset that rig did not build (one made by hand, or by another rig): add
  `physics_asset()` to the rig's outputs and name it here. An asset that rig built before it
  declared `dynamic=`: build the rig again so its physics asset carries it. Setting a drive on the
  asset by hand does not clear the refusal while the skeleton declares the joint. A declaration
  held only by a `controls:` adapter (MetaHuman), or by a parent rig whose skeleton a child rig
  shares, is not on the skeleton, so it is not seen: give such a body a child rig that builds its
  own physics asset.
- a rig-built asset with `gravity=true` (or `physics_profile=`) and nothing named: nothing would
  simulate; name the bodies that should simulate in `physics()`, or remove `gravity=` and
  `physics_profile=` from the clip.
- a physical `contact()` on a rig-built asset: it needs a Physical Animation profile with a position
  strength, which a rig does not write.

Name the whole part that should move. A simulated body whose child body is left kinematic is held
at that joint by the child, which stays on its animation pose (measured on the test rig: a thigh released with its calf left kinematic moved 0.000 degrees; with the calf simulated too, 73.9).
Every body the rig's physics asset has below another one is jointed to it, a twist helper's or a
rigid tip's included, so these belong to the part too; and a child declared `dynamic=0` holds its
parent the same way, so such a parent is relaxed only with the child given an amount above 0.

**This scope is the bake's.** The runtime `constraints()` output does not narrow: it has no clip
`physics()` to read and keeps simulating every body that is not explicitly kinematic. A clip with
`physics()` lines on such an asset says so in its build: `at runtime the whole body simulates; the
relaxed part's sway is in the baked keys`. The baked keys are the product.

### How far a part sways, by amount

Measured on a hand-sized part (a capsule 3 cm in radius, its centre 7 cm from the wrist) on an arm
swinging as a walking arm does, the shoulder 24.5 degrees and the elbow 28 at 0.87 Hz, with
`physics(hand, weight=0)` and `gravity=true` (`GraphCode.Move.Relax.AHandLagsASwingingForearm`,
its `walk` table). The swing is the hand's bend against the forearm, peak to peak. A walking wrist
bends 8.5 +- 3.2 degrees.

| `dynamic=` | swing of a hand-sized part | what it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 3.0 degrees | held: it barely leaves the forearm's line |
| 0.5 | 7.4 degrees | a walking hand: inside the measured 5.3-11.7, about 1 % of the stride late |
| 0.7 | 20.7 degrees | loose: a shaken-out hand, 3 % of the stride late |
| 0.8 | 32.3 degrees | very loose, flapping at the wrist |
| 0.9 | 42.4 degrees | nearly free: it hangs and flops (free at 1 would be 45) |

No resonance appears: gravity alone gives this hand a swing of about 1.4 Hz, above walking pace at every amount, so a
freer hand sways more, without a peak. A walking hand is about `dynamic=0.5` (0.45 to 0.6 spans the measured
range); at 0.6 it swings 12.3 degrees. Released half-way in a clip (`weight=50%`) the same hand at 0.5 swings
12.9 degrees instead of 7.4. The elbow alone shakes a hand four times less than the shoulder does (1.5
degrees against 6.2 at 0.5): it is the arm's swing from the shoulder that carries the hand.

The table holds for a hand; a longer part hangs on a longer lever under gravity and swings at its
own frequency, so measure it before reusing these numbers.

## Place a palm against the body

```gcmove
Acquire = profile(0=0, 0.35=1, 0.8=1, 1=0)
HandOnBody = pose(collision=self) {
  contact(arm_R, contact=Palm, on=self, facing=surface, weight=$Acquire)
}
Motion = clip(frames=120) { $HandOnBody }
```

`arm_R` and `Palm` are the character's existing IK chain and named rig contact. No target
socket is required. `on=self` searches enabled body collision surfaces outside the contacting
limb. `on=spine_03` restricts the target to that native body; `on=$Torso` accepts an existing
`Torso = bones(...)` group. These names are authored character data, not anatomy keywords.

Omit `facing` for natural touching: the named pad guides initial placement, and acquisition
selects the nearby touching point on that endpoint's native collider. It can touch on an edge
without flattening the whole pad. `facing=surface` requests the exact projected pad point and
opposes its outward normal to the target surface normal throughout the hold. Rotation around
that normal remains free; contact does not silently capture a full orientation lock. No particular palm bone axis is
assumed. Collider fit matters: this targets collision geometry, not exact deformed skin triangles.

The incoming evaluated pose is the starting form, including base movement and an already
placed parent contact. Contact does not reset the limb to the reference pose. It may bend joints
to reach the surface; their incoming angles are not locked. Weight zero preserves that form,
and `weight=profile(...)` follows the acquired joint form toward contact. This uses the approach
checked during acquisition, including its joint rotation, instead of pulling the pad along a
straight spatial line that can cross the body. The attached target still follows body motion;
full weight requests that target and zero weight releases to the current form. Explicit angle
instructions can refine the form while the same joint limits and collision checks remain authoritative.
Here, incoming means the evaluated form at the current frame before this contact is solved,
not the preceding line of text. Parent contacts are resolved before their descendant contacts,
so changing the declaration order does not change which pose the fingers start from.
An already touching angled fingertip need not be flattened in natural mode. Changing to
`facing=surface` adds a new requirement, so the same starting pose may then need adjustment.

```gcmove
# Natural touching from the incoming finger form.
contact(index_R, contact=Index, on=self, weight=$Acquire)
# Touching plus explicit pad alignment.
contact(index_R, contact=Index, on=self, facing=surface, weight=$Acquire)
```

These lines are alternatives, not two goals for the same pad. Older body-contact examples
implicitly aligned every pad; add `facing=surface` to retain that intention.

Different contacts in one pose can choose independently. A held, aligned palm and a naturally
touching finger use the same grammar:

```gcmove
HandOnChest = pose(collision=self) {
  contact(arm_R, contact=Palm, on=spine_03, facing=surface)
  contact(index_R, contact=Index, on=spine_03, weight=$Acquire)
  contact(middle_R, contact=Middle, on=spine_03, weight=$Acquire)
  contact(ring_R, contact=Ring, on=spine_03, weight=$Acquire)
  contact(little_R, contact=Little, on=spine_03, weight=$Acquire)
}
```

The pose's authored form supplies the starting arm and finger angles. Add `facing=surface` to
the finger only when its named pad must align too; contact does not imply a flat whole hand.
Contacts activating together are fitted together. The solver first uses joints outside already
held contact chains, then allows their participating ancestors when needed. An attained palm
cannot be traded away merely to reduce the combined finger error. All candidate movement still
passes the same native joint limits and collision checks.

Candidate selection occurs at activation; the acquired source point stays attached to its
endpoint, while the chosen target point and explicitly requested surface normal follow their
target bone until weight reaches zero. A later activation can acquire new points. The same
weight/profile, nested timing and pose contributions as reach apply. Self collision is included
even when omitted from the enclosing pose. Two goals cannot own the same position/facing channel.

The target body may move on its own. A palm resting on a thigh that lifts follows it, and returns to
the same place when the thigh does; freedoms the contact leaves open settle toward the authored form
instead of drifting. If the authored form leaves the limb unable to reach the moving spot (a straight
arm already on its elbow limit, for example), the build warns with the residual rather than forcing
it — bend the form to give the limb room. Without finger contacts or `fixate`, the palm may turn
about its own normal while the body part moves.

Unmet position/facing is reported after constraint correction. Collision and anatomical limits
take priority over forcing contact. Missing native coverage, excluded collision pairs and a
target inside the moving limb produce diagnostics. Gravity is optional. This variant bakes
ordinary animation tracks; it does not generate an Animation Blueprint or live-world acquisition.

**Verification and limitations:** the saved 3.2-second humanoid comparison now acquires the palm
and all four natural finger contacts, holds them through torso motion, and releases. Front and
grazing side sequences were inspected. After final correction, the palm attachment residual is
0.012 cm / 0.059 degrees and the largest finger residual is 0.003 cm. Independent saved source
and compressed playback checks cover 3,073 samples each and pass native collision checks.
The source passes the native joint-limit audit; compression introduces up to 0.0062 degrees of
measured limit drift, which the build reports after compression finishes. This comparison uses
the declared chest body and fitted native collision shapes; it does not establish exact deformed
skin contact for every character. Earlier rejected results remain in the project validation log.

Playback interpolates generated poses. The build can increase the bake rate to retain joint
limits between keys, but increasing the rate cannot repair an unattained contact. Warnings
identify the affected bone, measured correction or contact shortfall, and time/sample.

Explicit facing preserves an already attained bent finger form through partial blending in
the native composed-parent regression. The complete four-flat-pad chest comparison remains
unresolved. The unrestricted
`on=self` approach/lean/release example also has an outstanding attachment error. These are
not finished examples. Position/facing warnings identify unmet requests; syntax support is
not a guarantee that the current solver attains every composed goal.

### Finger form and finger contact in one pose

The two instructions express different intentions. A finger form requests bending; collision
stops it at the first obstruction. A named finger contact requests touching near that pad;
`facing=surface` requests the exact pad and alignment, and IK chooses the bends. Curling alone does not guarantee every fingertip reaches the
chest: a finger may encounter the palm or another finger first.

Both compose with a palm contact in the same `pose(collision=self)`. For example, the tested
humanoid form uses the rig's right-finger ranges as authored form data:

```gcmove
MCP = bones(index_01_r,middle_01_r,ring_01_r,pinky_01_r)
PIP = bones(index_02_r,middle_02_r,ring_02_r,pinky_02_r)
DIP = bones(index_03_r,middle_03_r,ring_03_r,pinky_03_r)
FingerForm = pose() {
  swing($MCP,axis=Z,by=-90)
  swing($PIP,axis=Z,by=-105)
  swing($DIP,axis=Z,by=-80)
}
Curl = profile(0=0,0.15=0,0.7=1,1=1)
HandOnChest = pose(collision=self) {
  contact(arm_R,contact=Palm,on=spine_03,facing=surface)
  $FingerForm@$Curl
}
```

Use that character's declared axes and limits; these bone names and angles are example data,
not a built-in fist. The validation fixture also declares the starting arm form. It leaves
the thumb uncommanded. No new `close()` or activity-specific verb is needed.

For the contact-driven alternative, a rig-defined finger pad can request
`contact(index_R,contact=Index,on=spine_03,weight=$Curl)` alongside the palm goal. Each pad
needs native endpoint collision coverage. `on=self` means all eligible surfaces outside that
chain's moving subtree; it does not mean "the chest" or inherit the palm's selected body.
Use the existing explicit body/group selector when that region matters. Simultaneous palm
and finger-pad contact exposed a parent/descendant ownership defect in humanoid validation;
the hierarchy correction passes native regression tests. The natural-touch comparison now
preserves the palm and visibly brings the four fingertips onto the chest, with the measured
little-finger settling residual reported above. Requiring every
exact named pad to align remains an unresolved humanoid case. The native incoming-form test
now verifies preservation through an explicit-facing blend. Unmet goals must warn.

When a pose declares contacts on both an ancestor and its descendant, the ancestor's contact
establishes the incoming pose first. Held contacts solve together: the active ancestor's declared
chain may contribute while its attained contact point and requested normal remain simultaneous
constraints. For example, a finger may need the palm's free turn around its contact normal.
The preceding constrained solution seeds the next frame, following changes in the incoming form;
the goal is still evaluated from this frame's weight, so influence does not accumulate. Fading
weight also releases the carried joint correction. Contact does not imply a full transform fixation.
At zero parent contact weight, the parent follows its authored form and the descendant cannot
take over that transform. This dependency comes from the skeleton, not instruction order; it
does not change which bodies `on=self` may target or edit the saved IK rig.

## Verifying a character

Inspect the Physics Asset against the rendered mesh first. A collider can stop correctly while skin
outside that collider still appears to enter the floor. Check fall, first impact, settling, each
supporting side, the transition between them and release. Inspect intermediate frames and repeat on
the actual target character; a primitive-body solver test is not humanoid visual acceptance.

Oversized colliders cause the opposite problem: visible skin floats above a correctly supported
physical body. Fit each supporting shape to the mesh in the relevant orientations. A single round
capsule may poorly represent a flattened foot or a broad torso; use native compound primitives where
needed. These shapes and muscle strengths belong to the character's Physics Asset, not hidden Move
defaults. Reopen the saved asset when checking joint limits as well as inspecting its live state.

Simulation uses the selected Physics Asset's bodies and joints. Inferred skinning coverage used by
pose fitting and final audits does not automatically become a simulated body. Include native
coverage for fingers, paws and other surfaces that can support the character. Otherwise simulation
can place a hand capsule legally while an uncovered fingertip enters the floor, forcing the final
audit to lift the character. Use articulated bodies when those parts move; a rigid compound shape
only represents the form it was fitted to. Do not duplicate that coverage across overlapping bodies.

The native rig's `GC_JointLimitExcess` curves report the largest input/physics correction in degrees.
The animation bake also audits the final tracks and interpolation. Do not treat a passive fall,
successful graph compilation or a still image as proof of a controlled roll.

Unreal's animation compression can change otherwise valid source rotations. Move audits the
compressed animation as well and warns about introduced joint-limit drift. A successful source
bake alone does not guarantee that the selected compression settings preserve every joint limit.
Keep those warnings visible when reviewing or shipping the resulting animation.
